# Rigorous LMIC and India evidence on improving school learning outcomes (for MIP ranking)

Notation: SD = standard deviations of test scores. LAYS = learning-adjusted years of schooling. ITT = intent-to-treat. "[Primary]" = original paper or its official abstract. "[Summary]" = J-PAL, World Bank, GEEAP or secondary summary. "[UNVERIFIED]" = figure from prior knowledge that I could not confirm from a source this session. Do not cite it without checking.

MIP feasibility tags, used per intervention: **LC** = low-cost; **DF** = device-free; **HT** = a head teacher or teacher can run it in a 2–8 week project; **SYS** = needs system-level support (state/district materials, money, HR, data systems).

## 1. GEEAP "Smart Buys" ratings (2020, 2023, and later)

### Takeaway
The 2023 GEEAP Smart Buys report rates three "Great Buys": (1) information on the benefits, costs and quality of education, (2) structured pedagogy, and (3) teaching by learning level rather than grade. It rates input-only spending (hardware alone, textbooks, extra teachers to cut class size, buildings, grants, salary, libraries) as "Bad Buys". The only later GEEAP report (Oct 2025) is on reading instruction. It does not re-rate the categories.

### Cited Findings
- **2023 report: "2023 Cost-Effective Approaches to Improve Global Learning: What does recent evidence tell us are 'Smart Buys'…"** [Primary, GEEAP brief]. The panel searched more than 13,000 additional studies and found more than 400 additional high-quality evaluations, bringing the total behind the categories to more than 550. Ratings weigh cost-effectiveness and whether the intervention has worked at scale. — [GEEAP 2023 PDF, World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099008110232520373/pdf/IDU-9401bfdd-f4ff-486b-84b4-570be4f65543.pdf)
- **Great Buys (2023)** are "highly cost-effective and supported by a strong body of evidence":
  - Providing information on the benefits, costs and quality of education.
  - Supporting teachers with structured pedagogy: a package of structured lesson plans, learning materials and ongoing teacher support.
  - Targeting teaching instruction by learning level, not grade, in or out of school.
  — [GEEAP 2023](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099008110232520373/pdf/IDU-9401bfdd-f4ff-486b-84b4-570be4f65543.pdf)
- **Good Buys (2023)**: parent-directed early childhood stimulation (ages 0–36 months); quality pre-primary education (ages 3–5); reducing travel times to schools; merit-based scholarships for disadvantaged children and youth; school-based mass deworming where worm load is high. — [GEEAP 2023](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099008110232520373/pdf/IDU-9401bfdd-f4ff-486b-84b4-570be4f65543.pdf)
- **Promising but Limited Evidence (2023)** (some rigorous studies show high effectiveness, but cost-effectiveness or scale evidence is lacking):
  - personalised adaptive learning software, where hardware is already in schools
  - augmenting teaching teams with community-hired staff
  - mass treatment for common health conditions (free eyeglasses, multiple micronutrients, preventive malaria treatment)
  - mobile phones to support learning
  - safeguarding students from violence
  - teaching socio-emotional and life skills
  - involving communities in school management
  - targeting interventions towards girls
  — [GEEAP 2023](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099008110232520373/pdf/IDU-9401bfdd-f4ff-486b-84b4-570be4f65543.pdf)
- **Effective but Relatively Expensive (2023)**: cash transfers (as a tool for learning); feeding in primary schools. — [GEEAP 2023](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099008110232520373/pdf/IDU-9401bfdd-f4ff-486b-84b4-570be4f65543.pdf)
- **Bad Buys (2023)** ("strong, repeated evidence shows that these programs have not worked … or are not cost-effective"):
  - investing in hardware like laptops, tablets and computers alone
  - funding additional inputs alone when other issues are not addressed: textbooks, additional teachers to reduce class size, school buildings, grants, salary, libraries
  — [GEEAP 2023](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099008110232520373/pdf/IDU-9401bfdd-f4ff-486b-84b4-570be4f65543.pdf)
- **Panel membership** includes Abhijit Banerjee (co-chair), Rukmini Banerji (Pratham CEO), Karthik Muralidharan and Benjamin Piper. Several panellists authored the India and Kenya RCTs below, so the panel is not fully independent of the evidence it rates. — [GEEAP 2023](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099008110232520373/pdf/IDU-9401bfdd-f4ff-486b-84b4-570be4f65543.pdf)
- **Category counts in summaries conflict.** The 2023 press release says 3 Great Buys, 5 Good Buys and 8 Promising. A World Bank feature says "eight Great and Good Buys". The brief itself lists 3 + 5 + 8, which matches the press release. — [WB press release, May 2023](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2023/05/09/education-smart-buys-cost-effectively-supporting-teachers-and-parents-can-lead-to-significant-learning-improvements); [WB feature, June 2023](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2023/06/21/evidence-based-cost-effective-learning-education-improvements)
- **GEEAP report history**: "Cost Effective Approaches to Improve Global Learning" (Oct 2020); "Prioritizing Learning During COVID-19" (Jan 2022); Smart Buys update (May 2023); "Effective Reading Instruction in Low- and Middle-Income Countries: What the Evidence Shows" (Oct 30, 2025, with a Hindi translation). The GEEAP page lists no 2024 report. — [GEEAP page, World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/brief/2025/10/30/global-education-evidence-advisory-panel)
- **2025 reading report** [Summary]:
  - Based on about 120 studies, about 50 of them in Africa.
  - Effective programmes teach decoding and language comprehension explicitly, systematically and comprehensively.
  - It names six core skills: oral language, phonological awareness, systematic phonics, fluency, comprehension and writing.
  - It recommends an appropriate language of instruction and structured training and materials for teachers.
  - Eight high-performing large-scale reading programmes (five in Sub-Saharan Africa) all used explicit instruction in subskills, including phonics-based decoding.
  — [WB press release, Oct 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/10/30/new-report-offers-evidence-based-solutions-to-address-global-literacy-crisis-among-children); [report PDF](https://www.developmentaid.org/api/frontend/cms/file/2025/10/IDU-740c63ca-259a-4fae-8d95-7935242a25db.pdf)

### Inferences
- For MIPs, the GEEAP Great Buys that a school can run itself are targeted instruction (grouping by level) and a light, school-run version of information to parents and students. Full structured pedagogy (guides, materials and coaching) is a SYS intervention. A school can only implement it faithfully if the state supplies the materials (as in NIPUN Bharat), not design it from scratch.
- Reading a "Bad Buy" correctly: an MIP that spends on inputs (library corner, TLM purchase, smart-class equipment) without changing instruction has repeated null evidence. Such MIPs should be reframed or dropped.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the 2020 report's category list to compare it with 2023. My recollection is that structured pedagogy and targeted instruction were not both Great Buys in 2020, but I did not verify this.
- The full 2023 report text (per-category effect sizes and cost ranges) was not extracted. Only the 2-page summary was.

## 2. Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) / Pratham

### Takeaway
TaRL has some of the largest rigorously measured effects in LMIC education. The effects depend heavily on the delivery model. Short, intensive "learning camps" in school hours gave 0.70 SD in Uttar Pradesh. A protected daily TaRL hour taught by government teachers with mentoring gave 0.15 SD in Haryana. Simply training government teachers, with no protected time or mentoring (Bihar, Uttarakhand), failed. Zambia's national government scale-up gave modest but real gains (0.10–0.15 SD over two years), held back by attendance.

### Cited Findings
- **Overall India evidence**: six randomised evaluations in seven Indian states since 2001. J-PAL calls the effects "some of the largest effect sizes rigorously measured in the education literature". Training teachers alone or providing materials alone did not improve learning. What worked was clear goals, use of data, mentoring and regular review meetings. [Summary] — [J-PAL TaRL scale-up](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/scale-ups/teaching-right-level)
- **Banerjee, Banerji, Berry, Duflo, Kannan, Mukerji, Shotland & Walton (2016), "Mainstreaming an Effective Intervention", NBER WP 22746** [Primary abstract]:
  - **Bihar and Uttarakhand (failed)**: government teachers did not adopt the method despite well-received training and Pratham support.
  - **Haryana (worked)**: government resource persons trained by Pratham supported teachers, who taught TaRL in a dedicated hour. Language gain 0.15 SD.
  - **Uttar Pradesh (worked)**: Pratham volunteers ran 40-day in-school "learning camps" during school hours, plus 10-day summer camps. Language gain 0.70 SD.
  - Effects apply to all baseline-enrolled students. A J-PAL summary of the same paper reports summer camps alone at 0.07–0.09 SD.
  — [NBER w22746](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22746); [paper PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22746/w22746.pdf)
- **UP learning camps**: the evaluation covered 17,266 students. Camps ran during school hours with regular teaching suspended, and doubled the share of children who could read a paragraph or story. [Summary] — [J-PAL TaRL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/scale-ups/teaching-right-level); [J-PAL remedial education page](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/scale-ups/remedial-education)
- **Camp model parameters**: camps of about 10 days, 3–5 camps a year (30–50 instructional days), children grouped by level for 2–3 hours a day. In the government-partnership model, teachers run TaRL for 1–2 hours a day with mentoring from government "leaders of practice". [Summary] — [J-PAL TaRL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/scale-ups/teaching-right-level)
- **Banerjee et al. 2007 QJE "Remedying Education" (Balsakhi + CAL, Mumbai and Vadodara)**: the reference is listed by J-PAL. — [doi 10.1162/qjec.122.3.1235](https://doi.org/10.1162/qjec.122.3.1235)
  - **[UNVERIFIED]** Commonly cited figures: Balsakhi (a community woman tutoring the lowest-performing grade 3–4 children outside class) raised scores by about 0.14 SD in year 1 and about 0.28 SD in year 2. Computer-assisted maths in Vadodara gave about 0.35 SD in maths in year 2. Gains faded substantially a year after the programme ended.
  - Angrist et al. list "CAL in India (Vadodara, yr 2)" among the above-median cost-effective interventions. — [Angrist et al. WWHGE WP 2023](https://www.wwhge.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/BSG-WP-2023-057-Improve-Education-Outcomes-small.pdf)
- **Jaunpur, UP volunteer reading camps** (Banerjee, Banerji, Duflo, Glennerster & Khemani 2010, AEJ: Policy; 280 villages, 2005–06) [Primary abstract]. Volunteers trained to run reading camps for children who could not read produced substantial gains in reading for attendees, with an average of 7.4 camps per village. (Information-only arms: see §4.) — [NBER w14311](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14311/w14311.pdf); [AEJ link via J-PAL](http://www.aeaweb.org/articles.php?doi=10.1257/pol.2.1.1); [Redivis dataset listing](https://stanford.redivis.com/datasets/dt6v-9q3zsh7f4)
- **Zambia Catch Up (de Barros & Lubozha; J-PAL; AEARCTR-0014922)** [Primary, J-PAL evaluation page]:
  - **Design**: cluster RCT of the government-run TaRL programme. 182 zones and 1,115 government primary schools randomised, analytic sample of 273 schools and 8,025 Grade 3 students, 2022–2024. Catch Up targets Grades 3–5.
  - **Two-year effects**: literacy +0.10 SD; numeracy +0.15 SD; targeted maths skills +0.40 SD; non-targeted maths near zero; working memory +0.07 SD.
  - **Long-term**: Grade 7 exams +0.14 SD (language) and +0.11 SD (maths), from an event study of about 4.4 million exam records.
  - **Teacher CPD add-on**: no additional gain.
  - **Attendance**: only about one third of students attended remedial class on a typical day.
  - **Cost**: US$9.63 per student over two years (US$19.97 with CPD).
  — [J-PAL Zambia evaluation](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/targeting-foundational-skills-improve-learning-scale-zambia)
  - The "+0.40 SD" figure circulating in TaRL Africa materials applies to targeted maths skills only, not overall learning. — [TaRL Africa Zambia RCT](https://teachingattherightlevel.org/zambia-rct/)
- **Côte d'Ivoire (PEC)**: a school-randomised trial (167 schools, 303 teachers, 3,808 students) tested TaRL, fortnightly assessments and digital teacher training sessions, with an MDE of 0.175 SD. **I did not find the SD estimates.** — [ResearchGate: Targeted Instruction with Mobile-based Support, Côte d'Ivoire](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382766652_Impacts_of_Targeted_Instruction_with_Mobile-based_Support_on_Teachers_and_Students_in_Rural_Cote_d'Ivoire); GPE KIX technical report on TaRL mentoring/monitoring in African systems — [GPEKIX PDF](https://www.gpekix.org/sites/default/files/2025-05/Final%20technical%20report%20Teaching%20at%20the%20Right%20Level%20Learning%20how%20to%20improve%20mentoring%20and%20monitoring%20support%20to%20teachers%20at%20scale%20in%20African%20government%20systems_0.pdf)
- **Scale**: by 2021 TaRL reached more than 1 million children in 12 African countries. Zambia Catch Up was in 1,900 schools, and Nigeria in more than 800 schools across 5 states. [Summary] — [J-PAL TaRL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/scale-ups/teaching-right-level)
- **Cost-effectiveness**: Angrist et al. estimate that targeted instruction in India yields "up to 3 to 4 additional LAYS per $100", equivalent to the whole India–Argentina system gap. "Teaching at the right level (gov-led), IND" appears among the above-median cost-effective interventions. — [Angrist et al., WWHGE WP 2023](https://www.wwhge.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/BSG-WP-2023-057-Improve-Education-Outcomes-small.pdf)

### Inferences
- **MIP fit: high.** TaRL-style grouping by level, using a simple oral assessment (ASER-type), for a protected 1–2 hours a day over a 4–8 week "camp" maps closely onto the UP and Haryana models. It is LC and DF. It is HT-implementable if the head teacher protects the time and has the assessment and activity materials. Pratham materials are openly available, and NIPUN Bharat already mandates level-based grouping in many states.
- The key failure mode in Bihar and Uttarakhand was training without protected time, mentoring and review. An MIP that only "orients teachers to TaRL" is likely to fail. The MIP should build in (a) a fixed daily slot, (b) baseline, midline and endline assessment, and (c) weekly head-teacher review.
- The camp (burst) model gave the largest effect (0.70 SD), which fits the 2–8 week MIP window unusually well. Note, though, that the UP camps were run by Pratham volunteers with Pratham supervision, not by teachers alone.
- Expect smaller effects than 0.70 SD from a school-run version. The government-teacher models gave 0.10–0.15 SD (Haryana, Zambia). Attendance is a binding constraint, so pairing TaRL with an attendance MIP is sensible.

### Gaps
- No numeric effect sizes were retrieved for Bihar or Uttarakhand (reported as no adoption, so presumably about zero), for the earlier "Read India" evaluations, for Ghana's Teacher Community Assistant Initiative (Duflo, Kiessel & Lucas), or for Côte d'Ivoire.
- I did not retrieve TaRL cost per child in India.

## 3. Structured pedagogy (teacher guides + student materials + coaching)

### Takeaway
Structured pedagogy is a GEEAP Great Buy. Meta-analytic averages are about 0.23 SD (language) and 0.14 SD (maths). Flagship bundles such as Kenya's Tusome gave 0.6–0.8 SD in early grades. Its effectiveness depends on the full package, and "training only" or "books only" variants do much worse. It is mostly a system-level intervention.

### Cited Findings
- **Snilstveit et al. 2016, 3ie systematic review**: structured pedagogy programmes averaged about 0.23 SD on language and 0.14 SD on maths [Summary of a review]. Sources disagree on the year (2015 vs 2016). — [World Bank "Successful Teachers, Successful Students" 2021](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/7b72550a6aec51f4e6fbbd31199d7b3b-0200022021/original/SuccessfulTeachers-Oct-5.pdf); [3ie SR Summary 7](https://www.3ieimpact.org/sites/default/files/2019-05/srs7-education-report.pdf); [RISE WP-024, Cilliers et al.](https://riseprogramme.org/sites/default/files/2020-11/RISE_WP-024_Cilliers_TeachingPractice.pdf)
- **Tusome (Kenya, national literacy programme, Classes 1–2)**: learning gains of 0.63 SD and 0.76 SD across Classes 1 and 2 [Summary]. The full package of books, training and coaching gave much larger effects than books plus training, or training alone. The earlier PRIMR pilot found a 15:1 school-to-coach ratio more cost-effective than 10:1. — [World Bank 2021](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/7b72550a6aec51f4e6fbbd31199d7b3b-0200022021/original/SuccessfulTeachers-Oct-5.pdf); [Piper et al. 2018, J. Educational Change](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10833-018-9325-4); [RISE presentation, Piper 2019](https://riseprogramme.org/sites/default/files/inline-files/Piper_20190523.pdf)
- **South Africa (Cilliers et al., RISE WP-024)**: an RCT comparing centralised training with on-site coaching found coaching gave 0.232 SD on reading, in line with the Snilstveit benchmark. — [RISE WP-024](https://riseprogramme.org/sites/default/files/2020-11/RISE_WP-024_Cilliers_TeachingPractice.pdf)
- **Angrist, Evans, Filmer, Glennerster, Rogers & Sabarwal**, "How to Improve Education Outcomes Most Efficiently? A Review of the Evidence Using a Unified Metric":
  - **Versions and scope**: WB Policy Research WP 9450 (2020); WWHGE WP 2023; Journal of Development Economics 2025, doi 10.1016/j.jdeveco.2024.103382. More than 200 impact evaluations in 52 countries.
  - **Top two categories**: targeted instruction and structured lesson plans with linked materials, PD and monitoring are "consistently effective and cost-effective with relatively low variation". They yield on average about 3 LAYS per $100.
  - **Specific programmes (LAYS)**: Tusome/PRIMR Kenya 1.04 LAYS; Argentina training + structured curriculum + coaching 0.81 LAYS; a Gambia combined pedagogy + para-teachers + targeted instruction programme 4.04 LAYS; phone tutorials targeted to level in Uganda 1.11 LAYS.
  - **Null results are common**: more than half of the interventions reviewed had null effects.
  — [WWHGE WP 2023 PDF](https://www.wwhge.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/BSG-WP-2023-057-Improve-Education-Outcomes-small.pdf); [IDEAS WP 9450](https://ideas.repec.org/p/wbk/wbrwps/9450.html)
- **Categories with zero median LAYS effect** in Angrist et al.: cash transfers; community involvement in school management (e.g. training for community members); additional inputs alone (textbooks, technology hardware, uniforms, school grants, class-size reduction without complementary reforms); general-skills teacher training. — [Angrist et al. WWHGE WP 2023](https://www.wwhge.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/BSG-WP-2023-057-Improve-Education-Outcomes-small.pdf)

### Inferences
- **MIP fit: partial.** An individual school cannot produce a scripted curriculum and materials. In Indian states where NIPUN Bharat already provides teacher guides and workbooks, however, an MIP can target **fidelity of use**. Examples: head-teacher classroom observation with a short checklist, peer coaching, and daily use of the teacher guide. That is the "ongoing teacher support" component, which is the piece most associated with the large effects. The device-free version is LC and DF, and is HT-feasible for coaching and observation. Materials are SYS.
- "General-skills teacher training" has a zero median effect. MIPs framed as one-off training workshops should be deprioritised.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Snilstveit's sample sizes or the Tusome RCT N directly (Piper, Destefano, Kinyanjui & Ong'ele 2018). I found no Indian RCT of state-run structured pedagogy (e.g. NIPUN materials) with published effect sizes; see §4.

## 4. India RCTs: technology, remedial, para-teachers, incentives, management, community, FLN

### Takeaway
India's strongest evidence is for instruction targeted to the child's level, whether human-delivered (TaRL, Balsakhi) or software-delivered (Mindspark: 0.37 SD maths in about 4.5 months; 0.20–0.22 SD at scale in Rajasthan government schools). Contract teachers and teacher performance pay raised scores by 0.15–0.27 SD. Management and planning reforms (MP school quality assessment) and community information campaigns (Jaunpur) had zero effect. No NIPUN Bharat RCT results were published as of the search.

### Cited Findings
- **Mindspark (Muralidharan, Singh & Ganimian 2019, AER, "Disrupting Education?")**: after-school adaptive software plus small-group instruction, Delhi, middle school.
  - ITT effects of 0.37 SD in maths and 0.23 SD in Hindi over the study period [Primary].
  - The paper says these gains were achieved in roughly a tenth of the time needed by comparison programmes, and were largest for the lowest achievers.
  - **[UNVERIFIED]** Sample of about 619 students in grades 6–9, with roughly 4.5 months of exposure.
  — [AER accepted version](https://econweb.ucsd.edu/~kamurali/papers/Published%20Articles/Disrupting_education_AER.pdf); [NBER w22923](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22923/w22923.pdf)
- **Mindspark at scale in Rajasthan government schools ("Adapting for Scale", NBER w34205, 2025)**: personalised adaptive learning built into the regular timetable, with the Government of Rajasthan and Educational Initiatives. Sample more than 20 times the original study, 18-month follow-up. Maths +0.22 SD and Hindi +0.20 SD, a 50–66% productivity increase over control. Gains were proportional to time on the platform. [Primary abstract] — [NBER w34205](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34205)
- **Contract (para) teachers, Andhra Pradesh (Muralidharan & Sundararaman, NBER w19440)**:
  - After 2 years, an extra contract teacher raised maths by 0.16 SD and language by 0.15 SD.
  - Contract teachers were less often absent (18% vs 27%) and more often actively teaching (48% vs 42%).
  - They were no less effective than regular teachers paid about 5 times more.
  - Sources differ on sample size (100 vs 200 schools).
  — [J-PAL summary](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/extra-contract-teachers-andhra-pradesh-india); [NBER w19440](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w19440/revisions/w19440.rev0.pdf)
- **Teacher performance pay, Andhra Pradesh (Muralidharan & Sundararaman 2011, JPE; APRESt)**:
  - 500 schools in 5 arms of 100 (control, extra contract teacher, block grant, group bonus, individual bonus), baseline 2005, 2 school years.
  - After 2 years, group bonus +0.16 SD and individual bonus +0.27 SD (0.28 SD in maths).
  - Bonuses averaged about 3% of annual salary and were more cost-effective than spending the same amount on inputs. [Summary]
  — [J-PAL summary](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/teacher-performance-pay-andhra-pradesh-india)
  - Five-year individual-bonus effects of 0.54 SD (maths) and 0.35 SD (language) are attributed to Muralidharan (2012). This comes from secondary citation inside the Mindspark paper and is not otherwise verified. — [NBER w22923](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22923/w22923.pdf)
  - Angrist et al. list "teacher incentives, IND" among above-median cost-effective interventions. — [Angrist et al.](https://www.wwhge.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/BSG-WP-2023-057-Improve-Education-Outcomes-small.pdf)
- **School governance / school development plans, Madhya Pradesh (Muralidharan & Singh, NBER WP 28129, 2020, "Improving Public Sector Management at Scale? Experimental Evidence on School Governance in India")**:
  - **Phase 1 RCT**: 2014–16, 1,774 schools. School assessments were almost universally completed and the ratings were informative, but there was **no effect** on school functioning or student learning.
  - **Scale-up**: the programme was later expanded to more than 600,000 schools nationally, and a matched evaluation of the scaled version also found no learning gains.
  - **Mechanism**: officials interviewed said it mainly added paperwork and reporting.
  — [RISE WP 20/056](https://riseprogramme.org/sites/default/files/2020-11/RISE_WP-056_Muralidharan_Singh.pdf); [VoxDev summary](https://voxdev.org/topic/education/improving-public-sector-management-scale); [J-PAL summary](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/node/4841173); [causal-claims summary](https://causalclaims.trfetzer.com/paper/w28129.html)
- **Community participation / information, Jaunpur UP (Banerjee et al. 2010 AEJ: Policy, "Pitfalls of Participatory Programs")**:
  - 280 villages, 2005–06. Arms: (1) information on Village Education Committee roles, (2) training communities in a reading-test tool, (3) volunteer reading camps.
  - Arms 1 and 2 had **no impact** on community involvement, teacher effort or learning. Arm 3 substantially improved the reading of children who attended. [Primary abstract]
  — [NBER w14311](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14311/w14311.pdf); [MIT PDF](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2022-08/pitfalls.pdf)
- **NIPUN Bharat / FLN evaluations**: I found **no published RCT effect sizes** for NIPUN Bharat. What exists is under way:
  - a registered cluster RCT in 240 government primary schools in Bulandshahr and Amroha, UP, testing extra mentoring vs standard UP-NIPUN implementation (IRB approval November 2024) — [AEA registry 17638](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/17638)
  - a 3-year (2022–25) mixed-methods/quasi-experimental evaluation in UP and MP comparing state-led vs district-level support models (CIES 2026 abstract, no numbers) — [CIES 2026 listing](https://convention2.allacademic.com/one/cies/cies26/online_program_direct_link/view_paper/2292950/)
  - LiftEd EdTech Accelerator RCT-style studies in Odisha, Rajasthan and UP (2026 reports; estimates not seen) — [LiftEd UP](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmfdkajkq/images/v1776772521/EdTech-Accelerator-UP-Digital-version/EdTech-Accelerator-UP-Digital-version.pdf)
  - a behavioural-science trial on teacher uptake of FLN instruction (registry document) — [AEA registry doc](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/versions/192795/docs/version/file)

### Inferences
- **High-value, MIP-feasible India-backed ideas (LC, DF, HT)**: (a) level-based grouping and remedial blocks (TaRL, Balsakhi, Jaunpur camps); (b) using community volunteers or local youth as remedial tutors (Balsakhi, Jaunpur reading camps, contract-teacher evidence); (c) head-teacher monitoring of teacher attendance and time-on-task, as a light school-level analogue of the accountability mechanism. Performance pay itself is SYS and politically controversial. I would not recommend it for MIPs.
- **Avoid MIPs that are mainly planning, paperwork or self-assessment** (school development plans, self-evaluation reports). The MP RCT is direct Indian evidence of zero effect at scale. Also avoid MIPs that are mainly community awareness meetings or information to SMCs (Jaunpur: zero).
- Mindspark-type adaptive software is a strong evidence case, but it needs devices and connectivity (not DF, SYS). GEEAP rates it only "promising, where hardware is already in schools". Consider it only for schools that already have working ICT labs.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the exact Balsakhi numbers, the 5-year performance-pay results (Muralidharan 2012 / JHR), or Mindspark sample sizes (see the [UNVERIFIED] flags above).
- Other Indian RCTs not covered here include: Kremer/Muralidharan teacher absence monitoring; Banerjee et al. on tracking; Rajasthan camera monitoring of NGO teachers (Duflo, Hanna & Ryan 2012 AER); Andrabi et al. report cards in Pakistan (a strong South Asian analogue for "information on school quality"); and the Muralidharan & Singh Rajasthan/MP FLN work.

## 5. Information to parents/students; SMS and phone nudges

### Takeaway
Information on the returns to education is a GEEAP Great Buy and among the most cost-effective interventions per dollar, but the evidence is mostly from outside India (Dominican Republic, Madagascar). Low-tech phone tutoring with parents (Botswana) gave about 0.12 SD at very low cost. SMS alone had little or inconsistent effect.

### Cited Findings
- **Jensen 2010, QJE 125(2):515–548**: Dominican Republic, eighth-grade boys. Perceived returns to secondary school were far below measured returns. Students told the measured returns completed 0.20–0.35 more years of school over the next four years (J-PAL rounds this to 0.2). The outcome is attainment, not test scores. — [EconPapers / QJE](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:qjecon:v:125:y:2010:i:2:p:515-548.); [J-PAL summary](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/print/pdf/node/1727)
- **Madagascar information on returns (Nguyen 2008)**: Angrist et al. single it out as an order of magnitude more cost-effective than the median intervention. **[UNVERIFIED]** The commonly cited test-score effect is about 0.2 SD. — [Angrist et al.](https://www.wwhge.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/BSG-WP-2023-057-Improve-Education-Outcomes-small.pdf)
- **Botswana "low-tech" remote instruction (Angrist, Bergman & Matsheng 2022, Nature Human Behaviour)**: RCT in about 4,500 households during COVID closures (primary grades). Combined SMS plus phone-call tutoring gave about 0.12 SD, which the authors convert to about 0.89 SD per US$100. SMS alone had little effect in the journal version, although the earlier working paper reported larger early effects (0.16–0.29 SD across arms). — [OpenDevEd record](https://docs.opendeved.net/lib/47QG6SMJ); [INEE working paper record](https://inee.org/pt/node/721796); [EconPapers WP](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:csa:wpaper:2020-13)
- **Uganda phone tutorials** targeted to learning level gave 1.11 LAYS (Angrist et al.). Angrist et al. rank "mobile phone instruction such as targeted tutoring" among the top categories by median effect. — [Angrist et al.](https://www.wwhge.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/BSG-WP-2023-057-Improve-Education-Outcomes-small.pdf)

### Inferences
- **MIP fit**:
  - A head-teacher-run session for parents and students (grades 6–10) on the earnings returns to completing secondary school, using credible Indian wage data, is LC, DF and HT. It is a direct analogue of a Great Buy. Note that the main effect is on attainment and dropout, not test scores.
  - Weekly teacher phone calls to parents of the lowest-level children with a short practice task is LC and HT. It needs basic phones (most households have them), not school devices.
  - SMS-only nudges have weak evidence. Do not rank them high.
- The evidence is weaker for Indian contexts specifically. Treat these as "promising, transferable" rather than "India-proven".

### Gaps
- I found no Indian RCT of information on returns, and no Indian RCT of SMS or phone nudges to parents with test-score outcomes, in this search.

## 6. Interventions that failed or are disputed in LMICs

### Takeaway
There are repeated nulls for input-only spending (textbooks, hardware alone, grants, class-size cuts, unconditional salary increases), for management and planning paperwork, and for community information campaigns. The best-documented single example is Indonesia's doubling of teacher pay, which did not raise learning.

### Cited Findings
- **GEEAP 2023 Bad Buys**: hardware alone; textbooks, extra teachers to reduce class size, buildings, grants, salary and libraries when other issues are not addressed. — [GEEAP 2023](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099008110232520373/pdf/IDU-9401bfdd-f4ff-486b-84b4-570be4f65543.pdf)
- **Angrist et al.**: cash transfers, community school-management training, inputs alone and general teacher training have a zero median LAYS effect. Class size and inputs are "tightly concentrated around zero". Textbooks helped only top-quintile students in Kenya ("providing textbooks (top quintile), KEN"). — [Angrist et al.](https://www.wwhge.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/BSG-WP-2023-057-Improve-Education-Outcomes-small.pdf)
- **Indonesia unconditional teacher salary increase (de Ree, Muralidharan, Pradhan & Rogers 2018, QJE 133(2):993–1039, "Double for Nothing?")**: an RCT of the certification-linked doubling of base pay for incumbent teachers. GEEAP lists "salary" among Bad Buys. **[UNVERIFIED detail]** As commonly reported, the pay rise improved teacher satisfaction and reduced outside jobs, but had no effect on teacher effort or student learning. — [IDEAS WB WP 8264](https://ideas.repec.org/p/wbk/wbrwps/8264.html); [NBER w21806](https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/21806.html)
- **India MP school governance**: null at 1,774 schools and at scale. — [RISE WP](https://riseprogramme.org/sites/default/files/2020-11/RISE_WP-056_Muralidharan_Singh.pdf)
- **Jaunpur information and community testing**: null. — [NBER w14311](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14311/w14311.pdf)
- **TaRL via teacher training alone (Bihar, Uttarakhand)**: not adopted, and no gains. — [NBER w22746](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22746)
- **Teacher CPD add-on to TaRL in Zambia**: no extra gain. — [J-PAL Zambia](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/targeting-foundational-skills-improve-learning-scale-zambia)
- **Disputed or high-variance categories**: community-hired staff, adaptive software and mobile phones are rated only "Promising" by GEEAP despite strong individual RCTs, because cost or scale evidence is thin. — [GEEAP 2023](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099008110232520373/pdf/IDU-9401bfdd-f4ff-486b-84b4-570be4f65543.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Draft MIP ranking by evidence, effect size and feasibility**:
  1. Level-based grouping / TaRL camps or daily hour: 0.15–0.70 SD in India; LC/DF/HT. Needs protected time and an assessment.
  2. Remedial tutoring of the lowest learners by volunteers or community members (Balsakhi, Jaunpur camps): LC/DF/HT, with a strong India evidence base.
  3. Fidelity-of-use coaching and observation of state-provided structured materials (NIPUN): structured pedagogy averages 0.23/0.14 SD; HT plus SYS materials.
  4. Information to students and parents on returns to schooling (secondary grades) and parent phone engagement: very high cost-effectiveness, evidence mostly non-Indian; LC/DF/HT.
  5. Head-teacher monitoring of teacher attendance and time-on-task: the accountability category ranks high in Angrist et al., but direct school-run evidence is indirect.
- **Deprioritise**: school development plans and self-assessment, SMC awareness meetings, one-off teacher training workshops, library or TLM or equipment purchases without instructional change, and device-only "smart class" projects.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm Indonesia's exact point estimates, laptops-alone RCT figures (e.g. One Laptop per Child, Peru: Cristia et al. 2017; no learning gains [UNVERIFIED]), or the Kenya textbook RCT figures (Glewwe, Kremer & Moulin 2009: no average effect [UNVERIFIED]). Verify these before citing.
- The tool-call budget was exhausted before I could check grade and age ranges for every study. Known ranges: Zambia Grade 3 (Catch Up covers Grades 3–5); Tusome Classes 1–2; Jensen grade 8. Mindspark (grades 6–9) and Balsakhi (grades 3–4) are [UNVERIFIED]. High-school (grades 9–12) RCT evidence in India is thin in this inventory.
