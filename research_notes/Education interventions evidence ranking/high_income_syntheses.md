Research method note: the EEF website (educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk) returns HTTP 403 to automated fetches. Snapshot dates are given per strand. Links point to the canonical EEF URL; the same page as archived is at `https://web.archive.org/web/2026/<EEF URL>`. ## 1. EEF Teaching and Learning Toolkit (UK): every strand, with impact, evidence padlocks and cost

### Takeaway
On the current (2025–2026) Toolkit, the strands that combine **high impact, very low cost and at least moderate evidence** are: **metacognition & self-regulation (+8 months, 4 padlocks)**, **reading comprehension strategies (+7, 3)**, **feedback (+6, 4)**, **oral language interventions (+6, 4)**, **peer tutoring (+6, 4)**, and **phonics (+5, 4; early reading)**. Mastery learning (+5) and collaborative learning (+5) also show high impact but rest on only 2-padlock evidence. **Repeating a year is negative (−2 months, very high cost)**, **setting/streaming is 0 months and harms lower attainers**, and **learning styles has insufficient evidence**, so no impact figure is shown and EEF warns it may harm progress.

### How to read the EEF metrics (primary source: EEF Toolkit Guide)
- **Months of progress to effect size (SD) conversion:** 0 months = −0.04 to 0.04; +1 = 0.05–0.09; +2 = 0.10–0.18; +3 = 0.19–0.26; +4 = 0.27–0.35; +5 = 0.36–0.44; +6 = 0.45–0.52; +7 = 0.53–0.61; +8 = 0.62–0.69; +9 = 0.70–0.78. Labels: +1–2 "low", +3–5 "moderate", +6–8 "high", +9 and above "very high" — [EEF Toolkit Guide (PDF, Feb 2023, file v3.0.0 2025)](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/documents/toolkit/toolkit_guide_2025_v3.0.0.pdf)
- **Padlocks (evidence strength):** the starting rating depends on the number of included studies: 1 padlock = 11–39 studies, 2 = 40–59, 3 = 60–79, 4 = 80–119, 5 = 120 or more. Fewer than 11 studies gives 0 padlocks, and no months figure is shown. Padlocks are then **deducted** if few studies are recent, many are not RCTs, many are not independently evaluated (developer-run evaluations "typically have larger impacts"), many ran in non-regular conditions (researcher-delivered), or there is large unexplained heterogeneity. Word labels: 1 = very limited, 2 = limited, 3 = moderate, 4 = extensive, 5 = very extensive — [EEF Toolkit Guide](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/documents/toolkit/toolkit_guide_2025_v3.0.0.pdf)
- **Cost (£ per class of 25 per year):** very low ≤ £2,000 (< £80 per pupil); low £2,001–5,000 (≤ £200 per pupil); moderate £5,001–18,000 (≤ £720 per pupil); high £18,001–30,000 (≤ £1,200 per pupil); very high > £30,000. Cost **excludes** existing staff time and existing resources, and counts only additional spend such as training, materials and cover — [EEF Toolkit Guide](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/documents/toolkit/toolkit_guide_2025_v3.0.0.pdf)
- Pupils are compared with similar pupils who did not receive the intervention. Included studies are all school-based (no lab studies) and have a comparison group — [EEF Toolkit Guide](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/documents/toolkit/toolkit_guide_2025_v3.0.0.pdf)

### Key implementation caveats EEF states on top strands (primary source: strand pages above)
- **Metacognition:** "+8 months ... however, it can be difficult to realise this impact in practice." Effective components are explicit teaching of plan–monitor–evaluate strategies, teacher modelling of thinking aloud, and promoting reasoning, argumentation and debate. Studies with more disadvantaged pupils showed high impact — [EEF metacognition](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/metacognition-and-self-regulation)
- **Reading comprehension:** strategies must be taught explicitly, with text at the right challenge level, and then applied across subjects. The evidence base is about language comprehension, not decoding or fluency — [EEF reading comprehension](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/reading-comprehension-strategies)
- **Feedback:** focus feedback on the task, the subject and self-regulation. Feedback about the person is least effective. Feedback should not be limited to written marking (verbal +7). Impacts are highest when feedback is teacher-delivered. Monitor workload — [EEF feedback](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/feedback)
- **Oral language:** interaction is key; evidence is strongest when integrated with the existing curriculum; the higher the proportion of disadvantaged pupils, the more effective it seemed — [EEF oral language](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/oral-language-interventions)
- **Peer tutoring:** most effective for **reviewing or consolidating** material, not introducing new material. "Training for staff and tutors is essential". Particularly effective for low-attaining tutees. Only 13 of 145 studies measured effects on tutors — [EEF peer tutoring](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/peer-tutoring)
- **Phonics:** must be explicit and systematic and matched to pupils' current phonemic awareness. It "improves the accuracy of the child's reading but not necessarily their comprehension" — [EEF phonics](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/phonics)
- **Collaborative learning:** structured tasks are required; groups of 3–5 with a shared goal work best; inter-group competition can help — [EEF collaborative learning](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/collaborative-learning-approaches)
- **Mastery learning:** "challenging to implement effectively"; requires a high success threshold before moving on, monitoring, and extra support for slower pupils — [EEF mastery learning](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/mastery-learning)
- **Homework:** EEF itself uses homework as its example of a high headline (+5) on very low evidence: "this estimate may be less reliable" — [EEF Toolkit Guide](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/documents/toolkit/toolkit_guide_2025_v3.0.0.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Low-cost, device-free, teacher-led fit for Indian government schools (2–8 week MIP):**
  - *Feedback* (verbal and task-focused): device-free, zero cost, and suits a 2–8 week cycle (e.g., a structured verbal-feedback routine). Effects are larger in primary.
  - *Metacognition* (plan–monitor–evaluate, think-alouds): device-free, but EEF warns the impact is hard to realise. It needs teacher modelling skill, so a short project should target one explicit strategy.
  - *Reading comprehension strategies* (e.g., reciprocal-teaching style predicting, questioning, clarifying, summarising): device-free and needs suitable texts. Good fit for Grades 3–8.
  - *Oral language* (structured talk, vocabulary): device-free. It fits multilingual classrooms if done in the language of instruction, and suits disadvantaged pupils.
  - *Peer tutoring* (cross-age or same-age, consolidation): device-free and leverages large class sizes. It needs tutor training and structured materials. Strongly suited to large Indian classes.
  - *Phonics*: early grades only, device-free. In India the script and language matter (alphasyllabaries such as Devanagari differ from English phonics), so transfer is plausible but the content needs adaptation.
- **Avoid as MIPs:** detention/retention-type, streaming, and "learning styles" projects. These are the EEF's clearest no-effect, harmful or unproven strands.
- Months of progress are inflated relative to large RCTs (see section 4). Treat "+6 months ≈ 0.45–0.52 SD" as an upper bound for what a short teacher-led project will achieve.

### Gaps
- **School uniform** strand: the archived page returned 403, so I could not verify it. From memory it is around 0 months on very limited evidence, but this is **unverified**.
- **Digital technology** strand: no page was found at slug `digital-technology`. EEF may have renamed or retired it, or moved it to a guidance report. Unverified.
- I could not confirm which ten strands changed in the **May 2025 update** or what the late-2025/early-2026 batches changed (see section 6).

## 2. What Works Clearinghouse (US IES) practice guides: recommendations rated "Strong"

### Takeaway
WWC "strong" ratings cluster around **explicit, systematic instruction**: phonemic awareness and decoding (K-3), decoding, fluency and comprehension routines for older struggling readers (Grades 4–9), all six recommendations for struggling elementary maths students (2021), explicit comprehension-strategy and vocabulary instruction, **quizzing to re-expose content and asking deep explanatory questions**, metacognitive monitoring and visual representations in maths problem solving, explicit writing-strategy instruction, and classroom behaviour routines (expectations, reminders, praise, behaviour-rating feedback). The rating describes the **strength of the causal evidence**, not effect size. The panel recommends every practice in a guide regardless of rating — [WWC foundational reading guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/wwc_foundationalreading_070516.pdf)

### Cited Findings (primary sources: WWC guide pages)

### Inferences
- WWC and EEF converge strongly on: **explicit phonics and decoding (early grades)**, **explicit comprehension-strategy instruction**, **metacognitive self-monitoring** (WWC maths problem solving maps to EEF metacognition), **retrieval quizzing** (WWC Strong; this is a form of EEF feedback and metacognition), and **visual representations and the number line** in maths.
- Almost all WWC "Strong" practices are **device-free and teacher-executable in weeks**: a daily 5-minute retrieval quiz; a number-line routine; a word-problem schema routine; a classroom expectations, reminders and praise routine; explicit decoding blocks for Grades 3–8 children who cannot read (highly relevant given ASER-type foundational gaps in India).
- The 2024 behaviour guide gives a low-cost, non-controversial classroom-management MIP with Strong evidence (positive praise and expectations; note it rules out corporal punishment by design).

### Gaps
- WWC guides give evidence levels, not pooled effect sizes, so they cannot rank by magnitude. Individual studies with effect sizes are in each guide's appendix, which I did not extract.
- The Preventing Dropout (2017) and English Learners (2014) guide ratings were not extracted.
- IES/NCEE budget and staff cuts in 2025 may affect WWC updates. I did not verify the current status.

## 3. Hattie's Visible Learning: top influences and methodological criticisms

### Takeaway
Hattie's lists rank "collective teacher efficacy" (d = 1.57 in the 2017–2018 list; later revised downward, e.g., 1.39 in 2019) and student self-reported grades/expectations at the top, with feedback (~0.70–0.73), reciprocal teaching (~0.74), teacher clarity (0.75), spaced practice (0.71) and metacognitive strategies (0.69) high. Against a "hinge point" of d = 0.40, these numbers are **not comparable** to RCT effect sizes. The method averages meta-analyses of mixed quality, overlapping studies and different designs, and it converts correlations to d. Leading methodologists (Slavin, Simpson, Wecker et al., Bergeron, Snook et al., Terhart) argue the rankings are unreliable. **Use Hattie only as directional, never for magnitudes.**

### Cited Findings
- *Visible Learning: The Sequel* (Routledge, 2023) synthesises more than 2,100 meta-analyses, more than 130,000 studies and more than 400 million students — [Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/9781032462035); [The Conversation (Hattie)](https://theconversation.com/education-expert-john-hatties-new-book-draws-on-more-than-130-000-studies-to-find-out-what-helps-students-learn-201952)
- Hattie's "hinge point": the average effect across all influences is d = 0.40, and influences are judged relative to it — [visible-learning.org ranking (secondary, Hattie-affiliated)](https://visible-learning.org/hattie-ranking-influences-effect-sizes-learning-achievement/)
- Dec 2017 ranking: **collective teacher efficacy 1.57**, self-reported grades 1.33, teacher estimates of achievement 1.29, self-efficacy 0.92, teacher clarity 0.75 — [visible-learning.org (secondary)](https://visible-learning.org/hattie-ranking-influences-effect-sizes-learning-achievement/). CTE 1.57 traces to a single meta-analysis (Eells 2011 dissertation) — [Corwin Connect](https://corwin-connect.com/2016/07/fostering-collective-teacher-efficacy-three-enabling-conditions/). A June 2019 list gives CTE as 1.39 — [blog (secondary)](https://gettingbetterthinklearnshare.wordpress.com/visible-learning-top-10-from-2018-17-list-of-hatties-effect-sizes-related-to-student-achievement/)
- 2009 original list (as summarised by Wikipedia, a secondary source): self-reported grades 1.44, Piagetian programs 1.28, formative evaluation of programs 0.90, microteaching 0.88, acceleration 0.88, classroom behavioural 0.80, teacher clarity 0.75, **reciprocal teaching 0.74, feedback 0.73**, teacher–student relationships 0.72, **spaced vs massed practice 0.71, metacognitive strategies 0.69**, vocabulary programs 0.67, repeated reading 0.67 — [Wikipedia: Visible learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_learning)
- **Hattie admitted the CLE (common language effect size) values in *Visible Learning* were calculated wrongly throughout. Only the d values are correct** (raised by Topphol 2011) — [Wikipedia: Visible learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_learning)
- **Slavin (2018):** Hattie averages meta-analyses that include studies of all quality levels. Individual rigorous studies almost never reach +0.40 except one-to-one tutoring. Feedback's +0.79 rests on 12 meta-analyses, including one (Skiba et al., +1.24) based on 35 students in 25 single-subject studies, and one with a single two-student study at d = 11.81 (winsorised to 4.99). One included meta-analysis (Rummel & Feinberg, +0.60) actually reflected a **negative** effect of feedback. Slavin argues "+0.15 from rigorous studies" beats "+0.79 from flawed ones" — [Slavin blog, "John Hattie is wrong" (secondary commentary by a primary researcher)](https://robertslavinsblog.wordpress.com/2018/06/21/john-hattie-is-wrong/)
- **Simpson (2017),** *Journal of Education Policy* 32(4):450–466: standardised effect sizes are open to researcher manipulation (comparison group, sample restriction, outcome measure), which violates the assumptions needed to compare and combine them. League tables of interventions "may, instead, be hierarchies of openness to research design manipulations" — [Durham Research Online (accepted manuscript)](https://dro.dur.ac.uk/20605); [Durham repository](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1367527/)
- **Wecker, Vogel & Hetmanek (2016 online / 2017 print),** "Visionary and impressive – but also reliable? A critique of the methodology of Hattie's visible learning", *Zeitschrift für Erziehungswissenschaft* 20(1):21–40, doi:10.1007/s11618-016-0696-0. Reported conclusion: a large proportion of the findings are subject to reasonable doubt — citation from [reference list](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/ref/10.1080/01596306.2018.1480474); conclusion as quoted by a blog: [visablelearning.blogspot (secondary)](http://visablelearning.blogspot.com/p/effect-size.html). **Not verified against the original text.**
- **Bergeron (2017; trans. Rivard),** *McGill Journal of Education* 52(1): a statistician argues Hattie's methodology (e.g., converting correlations to d, averaging heterogeneous effects) amounts to "pseudoscience". The English version translates a 2016 French original — [MJE article](https://mje.mcgill.ca/article/view/9475); [Érudit](https://id.erudit.org/iderudit/1040816ar)
- **Snook et al. (2009, 2010) and Terhart (2011):** "garbage in, garbage out" (indiscriminate inclusion of poor-quality studies), publication bias / file-drawer problems, and loss of context — [Bath Dept. of Education working paper reviewing these critiques](https://www.bath.ac.uk/publications/department-of-education-working-papers/attachments/critique-of-peer-reviewed-articles-on-john-hatties-use-of-meta-analysis-in-education.pdf)
- Schulmeister & Loviscach (2014) document wrongly categorised studies and "misleading or nonsensical statistics" — [Wikipedia: Visible learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_learning)
- Retraction Watch reportedly ran a March 2026 headline saying the University of Melbourne opened a formal investigation into Hattie. I saw this only as a search-snippet headline and **did not verify** it or learn its scope — [search surfaced via John Hattie (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hattie)

### Inferences
- Hattie's top items that **converge with EEF/WWC** (feedback, reciprocal teaching, metacognition, spaced practice, teacher clarity, vocabulary and repeated reading) are reasonable picks. Their magnitudes should come from EEF or RCTs, not Hattie.
- "Collective teacher efficacy" is a correlational construct (teacher beliefs correlating with achievement), not a tested intervention. It should **not** be ranked as a top MIP based on d = 1.57.
- "Self-reported grades" (student expectations) is likewise a correlate, not an intervention.

### Gaps
- Exact current (2023 Sequel / MetaX) values for collective teacher efficacy, feedback and reciprocal teaching were not verified. Values change across editions (CTE 1.57 to 1.39 or lower).
- Wecker et al.'s specific quantitative findings (e.g., the share of meta-analyses with design problems) were not read in the original.

## 4. Interpreting effect sizes: Kraft (2020), Lortie-Forgues & Inglis (2019), Cheung & Slavin (2016)

### Takeaway
For causal studies with standardised achievement outcomes, Kraft's benchmarks are **< 0.05 SD small, 0.05–< 0.20 SD medium, ≥ 0.20 SD large**. Large, independently evaluated, preregistered RCTs (EEF and US NCEE) average only about **0.06 SD**. Small samples, researcher-made tests and quasi-experiments roughly double effect sizes. So EEF "months" (built from all study types) and Hattie's d values overstate what a typical scaled programme delivers.

### Cited Findings
- Kraft (2020), "Interpreting Effect Sizes of Education Interventions", *Educational Researcher* 49(4):241–253, doi:10.3102/0013189X20912798. Effects that are small by Cohen's standards are large relative to most field interventions. It proposes five guidelines (account for study features, outcome type, cost and scalability) and new empirical benchmarks — [EdWorkingPaper ai19-10](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai19-10); [Annenberg](https://annenberg.brown.edu/publications/interpreting-effect-sizes-education-interventions)
- Kraft's benchmarks: **< 0.05 SD small, 0.05 to < 0.20 medium, ≥ 0.20 large** — [Shanker Institute summary (secondary)](https://www.shankerinstitute.org/node/5849)
- Kraft (2023), *Educational Researcher* 52(3):183–187: **36% of effect sizes from RCTs of education interventions with standardised achievement outcomes are below 0.05 SD**. "Education interventions often fail" — [Kraft site](https://www.matthewakraft.com/the-effect-size-benchmark-that-matters-most-education-interventions-often-fail)
- **Lortie-Forgues & Inglis (2019),** *Educational Researcher*: **141 large-scale RCTs** commissioned by EEF (UK) and NCEE (US), **1,222,024 students**, K-12. Average effect **≈ 0.06 SD**, with wide confidence intervals. About 40% of trials were judged uninformative (median Bayes factor 0.56) — [White Rose eprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/141754/); [Loughborough](https://dspace.lboro.ac.uk/dspace-jspui/handle/2134/36789). A UCL technical comment cites a weighted mean of **0.04** — [UCL comment (PDF)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10101577/1/Informing%20better%20trial%20design%20-%20A%20Technical%20Comment%20on%20Lortie-Forgues%20and%20Inglis%202019.pdf). Simpson contested the Bayes-factor prior; the authors replied — [response to Simpson](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/150840/1/ResponseToAdrianFINAL.docx)
- **Cheung & Slavin (2016),** "How Methodological Features Affect Effect Sizes in Education", *Educational Researcher* 45(5):283–292. **645 studies**. **Small-sample studies had about twice the effect sizes of large-sample studies.** Researcher-made measures, quasi-experiments and published studies all had higher effect sizes than independent measures, randomised experiments and unpublished studies. Each factor had an independent effect in a regression — [bestevidence.org summary (secondary)](https://bestevidence.org/2021/02/07/methods-methodological-features-and-effect-sizes/); [Bohrium record](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/how-methodological-features-affect-effect-sizes-in-education/814510827255103489-12366)
- A 2024–2026 EdWorkingPaper (Gilbert & Soland) finds researcher-developed outcome measures show larger effects than independent ones, partly explained by item-level over-alignment — [EdWorkingPaper ai24-1082](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai24-1082_v2.pdf)
- EEF itself states that developer-led evaluations "typically have larger impacts" and deducts padlocks for them — [EEF Toolkit Guide](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/documents/toolkit/toolkit_guide_2025_v3.0.0.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Practical rule for MIP ranking:** discount meta-analytic averages (EEF months, Hattie d) by roughly 2–5x when predicting a scaled, teacher-run, short project. On Kraft's scale, a realistic **0.05–0.20 SD gain is a "medium", worthwhile result**; ≥ 0.20 SD is rare outside intensive tutoring and foundational reading.
- A 2–8 week MIP measured with a teacher-made test will tend to **over-report** gains (Cheung & Slavin). Where possible, use an independent or standard assessment (e.g., ASER or ORF-style tools) to judge MIP success.

### Gaps
- I did not read Kraft's full text. The dataset size (from memory, about 1,942 effect sizes from 747 RCTs, median ≈ 0.10 SD) and his cost benchmarks are **unverified**.
- The exact Cheung & Slavin mean effect sizes per feature (e.g., small vs large sample means) are unverified.
- I could not resolve the 0.06 vs 0.04 discrepancy in Lortie-Forgues & Inglis (it likely reflects unweighted vs weighted averaging).

## 5. High-dosage tutoring: Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan

### Takeaway
Across RCTs of PreK-12 tutoring, the pooled effect is **0.37 SD (2020 working paper)**, revised to **0.29 SD in the 2024 peer-reviewed version (89 RCTs)**. This is far above the 0.06 SD typical of large RCTs, making tutoring one of the few interventions with consistently "large" effects on Kraft's scale. Effects are larger for teacher and paraprofessional tutors, in-school delivery, and earlier grades.

### Cited Findings
- 2020 working paper (NBER w27476 / EdWorkingPaper ai20-267): pooled effect **0.37 SD**, "consistent and substantial positive impacts". Larger effects for **teacher and paraprofessional tutors** than for nonprofessional or parent tutors. **In-school tutoring larger than after-school.** Effects strongest in **earlier grades**. Reading and maths similar overall: reading effects larger in early grades, maths effects larger in later grades — [EdWorkingPaper ai20-267](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai20-267); [NBER w27476](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476)
- Published version: "The Promise of Tutoring for PreK–12 Learning: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Experimental Evidence", *American Educational Research Journal* (online Nov 2023): **pooled 0.288 SD (SE 0.029), 89 RCT studies** — [SAGE AERJ](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00028312231208687) (figures from a search snippet; the page was 403 to fetch); [Stanford NSSA summary](https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/promise-tutoring-prek-12-learning-systematic-review-and-metaanalysis-experimental-evidence)
- EEF comparators: one-to-one tuition **+5 months** (moderate cost, 3 padlocks; primary +6, secondary +4); small group tuition **+4** (low cost, 3 padlocks; primary +4, secondary +2); peer tutoring **+6** (very low cost, 4 padlocks) — EEF strand pages in section 1.

### Inferences
- Paid adult high-dosage tutoring is costly. But the moderators (in-school, during the school day, structured, by paraprofessionals or teachers) point to **structured in-school small-group or peer tutoring** as the low-cost Indian adaptation. Using volunteers or parents gives smaller effects, so a MIP should use structured scripts and a trained tutor role, e.g., grouping by level in a TaRL-style approach (TaRL is outside this note's scope).
- Fits a 2–8 week MIP if run daily (high dosage, e.g., 3 or more sessions per week) during school hours.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the reason for the 0.37 to 0.29 revision or the published moderator coefficients.
- Some included tutoring RCTs are from low- and middle-income countries. I did not check how many.

## 6. 2023–2026 updates or replications that changed ratings

### Takeaway
EEF moved to rolling annual strand updates. In **May 2025** it updated ten strands, raising one approach (metacognition) from **+7 to +8 months**. Oral language interventions, published as **+6 months, 4 padlocks**, is now among the top strands. As of the Aug–Oct 2026 snapshots, the live figures are those in the section 1 table. Nickow et al.'s tutoring estimate fell from 0.37 to 0.29 SD in peer review. Hattie's figures were revised in the 2023 Sequel amid continuing criticism.

### Cited Findings
- May 2025: EEF launched comprehensive updates to ten Toolkit strands, including metacognition and oral language. The estimated impact of one approach increased "from an additional seven months' progress to eight months" (metacognition, consistent with the live page now showing +8) — [EEF news: updates to ten strands](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/updates-ten-strands-toolkit) (via search snippet; site 403 to fetch)
- The remaining strands were scheduled for update in two batches in late 2025 and early 2026, with all strands then refreshed with new searches yearly — [EEF Toolkit explainer v1.2.0 (PDF)](https://d10a08pz293654.cloudfront.net/production/documents/teaching_and_learning_toolkit_-_explainer_v.1.2.0.pdf) (via search snippet)
- The 2021 Toolkit relaunch had previously put metacognition at +7 (down from +8 in the older Sutton Trust-EEF toolkit) — [EEF blog: latest updates](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/latest-updates-to-the-toolkit) (via search snippet). A third-party search summary claimed a fall from +8 to +7, which matches the 2021 change, not the current figure.
- Feedback lost one padlock because many studies are not RCTs. It now shows 4 padlocks (155 studies) — [EEF feedback](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/feedback)
- Every live strand page carries the banner "minor editorial changes are currently being carried out on this Toolkit" (Aug–Oct 2026 snapshots) — e.g., [EEF metacognition](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/metacognition-and-self-regulation)
- Learning styles now displays "Unclear impact ... insufficient evidence", with no months shown — [EEF learning styles](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/learning-styles)
- WWC: the newest K-12 guides are Reading Interventions Grades 4–9 (2022) and Teacher-Delivered Behavioral Interventions K-5 (2024) — [WWC practice guides](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuides)

### Inferences
- **Synthesis ranking for MIP selection** (combining EEF magnitude, WWC Strong ratings, low cost, no devices, and a teacher-led 2–8 week fit):
  1. **Explicit phonics / decoding for non-readers**: EEF +5, 4 padlocks; WWC Strong for K-3 and Grades 4–9. Very low cost. Age-specific to early grades or remedial use. Script adaptation is needed for Indian languages.
  2. **Structured peer tutoring** (consolidation, trained tutors, low attainers as tutees): EEF +6, 4 padlocks, very low cost. Tutoring meta-analysis 0.29–0.37 SD. Suits large classes.
  3. **Feedback (verbal, task-focused) plus retrieval quizzing**: EEF +6, 4 padlocks; WWC Strong for quizzing. Zero cost. Larger effects in primary.
  4. **Reading comprehension strategies / reciprocal-teaching routine**: EEF +7, 3 padlocks; WWC Strong (K-3, adolescent literacy, Grades 4–9). Grades 3 and up.
  5. **Metacognitive plan–monitor–evaluate routines** (including maths problem-solving self-monitoring): EEF +8, 4 padlocks; WWC Strong (Grades 4–8 maths). Harder to implement well.
  6. **Oral language / structured classroom talk and vocabulary**: EEF +6, 4 padlocks; WWC Strong (vocabulary, adolescent literacy). Strongest for disadvantaged pupils.
  7. **Maths: number line, concrete representations, word-problem routines, short timed fluency practice for strugglers**: WWC Strong (all six, K-6).
  8. **Classroom behaviour routines** (expectations, reminders, praise, behaviour-rating feedback): WWC Strong (K-5, 2024); EEF behaviour +3, 3 padlocks.
  - **Avoid:** retention, streaming / ability tracking, learning styles, and class-size reduction (very high cost for +1).

### Gaps
- I could not confirm which ten strands changed in May 2025, or the specific changes in the late-2025/early-2026 batches. EEF news pages block automated access.
- I found no large 2023–2026 replication overturning a top EEF strand. EEF's own trial reports on specific programmes (e.g., individual metacognition or oral language programmes) were not reviewed here.
