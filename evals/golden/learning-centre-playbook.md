# Golden Example 4 — Learning Centre "Neighbourhood month" with interest circles (Connected Learning: APPLY, targeted)

> **Role in the eval set:** the reference case for Connected Learning's **interests** and **connections across settings** principles, applied to a design that is already strong on hands-on, peer and inquiry learning. It tests whether the transformer gives credit for what's there and adds only the thinnest parts.
> Like Example 3, it shows the **resource → MIP** step: the Play Book is a year-long calendar, so the baseline MIP is derived from one month of it (August).
> **Source:** Shikshagraha Commons resource **#753**, "Learning Centre Play Book" (Vidhya Vidhai Foundation; *Katral Koodam* Learning Centres, Coimbatore, CSR initiative; PDF, 72 pp., mostly Tamil). Load the original via `evals/sources.md`.

## The resource (summary)

- **Purpose:** Learning Centres respond to students *gradually disengaging* before they drop out. They're meant as a supportive space, not an extra classroom or burden.
- **Four features:** a purpose-driven space with subject and expression corners; flexible, self-paced learning; rich resources and displays of student work; monthly programmes, including **inviting local experts** (scientists, artists, doctors, entrepreneurs) and small projects (a school map, a water-use survey).
- **12 activities** (30–45 minutes, ages 6–18), including **Dream Job Map** and **Students as Teachers**.
- **4 strategies:** FIDS (Feel–Imagine–Do–Share); cross-curricular / phenomenon-based, with **Neighbourhood Explorer** and a **Neighbourhood Learning Wall**; Student-as-Explorer; experiential / inquiry.
- **Yearly calendar** (April–March). **August** pairs Neighbourhood Explorer with Dream Job Map under the phenomenon-based strategy. The year ends with an exhibition of student work.
- **Observation framework** for teachers.

## Already present (credit before adding)

Production (storybooks, models, wall, exhibition) · peer support (Students as Teachers, Reflection Partner) · connections to place (Neighbourhood Explorer, invited experts) · career opportunities (Dream Job Map).
**Thinnest parts:** student *choice* over time (activities follow the calendar); *everyday* community knowledge (experts are framed as distinguished professionals); families as audience.

## Transformed MIP

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Title** | Neighbourhood Month: interest circles and everyday mentors |
| **Target stakeholder** | Learning Centre teacher-in-charge (stays accountable), with HT approval for visitors |
| **Duration** | 6 weeks |
| **Objective** | *Unchanged:* connect learning to surroundings and careers. *Added:* every student, and especially those flagged as low-engagement, pursues one self-chosen interest in a circle and has one contact with an everyday community knowledge holder linked to it. |
| **Extra cost** | Nil (existing materials; volunteers unpaid) |

| # | Task | Change | Principle | Student role / artefact | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prepare materials; **flag low-engagement students** using the observation framework | modified | — | — | Flags are kept private by the teacher |
| 2 | Run Dream Job Map, **adding "one thing I like doing or want to learn"**; form **interest circles** of 3–5 | modified | Interest-powered | Each student names an interest | Everyone joins a circle; flagged students are not grouped together |
| 3 | **Circle choice periods** (fortnightly): each circle picks or adapts one Play Book activity around its interest | **added** | Interest-powered / sponsorship | Circle plan card | Uses only existing Learning Centre materials |
| 4 | Neighbourhood Explorer: **each circle interviews one everyday knowledge holder** (e.g. a tailor, mechanic, farmer, shopkeeper, health worker, or a parent with a craft) on a teacher-accompanied walk or supervised school visit | modified | Connections across settings | Interview notes on a question card | HT approves visitors; supervised, common areas, no one-to-one adult–child time |
| 5 | Neighbourhood Learning Wall: **each circle adds an artefact linking interest, mentor and a school subject** | modified | Production-centred | Artefact per circle | — |
| 6 | **Family showcase:** circles present at a PTM or open day; mentors invited | **added** | Connections across settings | Presenters rotate | No photos of children shared outside school without written parental consent |
| 7 | Record engagement in the observation framework, **with two added rows**: *student chose their activity*, *community connection made* | modified | — | Students co-maintain a progress board | Teacher remains the MIP owner |

**Size check:** 7 tasks (≤9) · 2 added (≤2) · 6 weeks (≤8) ✔

**Equity guardrail (whole MIP):** don't steer students toward trades associated with their family's caste or gender. Offer each circle a mix of mentors and let students choose.

### Success indicators
- ≥80% of flagged students attend ≥70% of circle periods.
- Every circle completes an interview and an artefact.
- Observation-framework engagement ratings for flagged students improve from week 1 to week 6. *(The link to dropout is a hypothesis, not a claim.)*

### Rationale (supportable claims only)
The resource's own diagnosis is gradual disengagement. Connected Learning holds that engagement is sustained when learning connects a young person's interests with relationships and opportunities, including adults who take those interests seriously (reference card: CL-interests, CL-relationships, CL-connections-across-settings). The resource already supplies the "doing" and the "sharing"; this MIP adds **choice** and **everyday community relationships**.
