# Golden Example 7 — Catalogue MIP "Real World Projects" (Connected Learning: APPLY; PB: ABSTAIN)

> **Role in the eval set:** the reference case for Connected Learning applied **directly to a catalogue MIP** owned by a teacher, the largest group in the catalogue (~31 pedagogy MIPs). It tests whether the transformer turns students from beneficiaries into choosers, peers and presenters **within the same task skeleton**.
> **Source:** MI catalogue (Commons #259), sheet *Cur & Ped & Ass*, category *Teaching learning processes / Pedagogy and subject knowledge of learners*, "Real World Projects" (maths teachers, 1 month).

## Expected gate decisions

| Gate | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Connected Learning | **APPLY** | Already production-centred (project + showcase). The teacher chooses the problems and students are recipients, so interest, peer roles and audience are thin. |
| PB process | **ABSTAIN** | No shared resource to allocate. |
| Budget-capped choice | **ABSTAIN** | Students choose a problem from options, but there's no hard cap. Use simple choice, not a knapsack. |

## Baseline MIP (catalogue row, paraphrased)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Title | Real World Projects |
| Domain | Curriculum, Pedagogy & Assessment |
| Target stakeholder | Maths teachers |
| Duration | 1 month |
| Objective | Teachers use real-world projects in class to build practical application of maths and problem-solving |

|---|---|

| 2 | Create a few real-life problem projects aligned to those concepts |

## Already present (credit before adding)

Production-centred (projects), academically oriented (tied to concepts), a showcase (audience within school).
**Gaps:** the teacher creates the problems (no student interest), no structured peer roles, the audience is the school only, and there's no link to adults who use this maths.

## Transformed MIP

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Title** | Real World Projects: students pick the problem |
| **Target stakeholder** | Maths teacher (unchanged) |
| **Duration** | 1 month (unchanged) |
| **Objective** | *Unchanged:* practical application of maths and problem-solving. *Added:* each team works on a real-life problem it chose from its own interests and explains it to a family audience. |
| **Extra cost** | Nil |

| # | Task | Change | Principle | Student role / artefact | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Decide the concepts to cover | unchanged | — | — | — |
| 2 | Prepare **3–4 project frames** for the concepts (e.g. "measure and compare", "track and chart over time", "cost and budget"). **Each team proposes a real-life problem from home or the neighbourhood that fits a frame** (e.g. cricket scores, shop prices, rainfall and crop timing, bus times) and the teacher approves it. | modified | Interest-powered | Team problem card | Every problem must hit the chosen concepts. No collection of family income or other sensitive household data. |
| 3 | Introduce the projects and timelines; form teams of 4–5 with **rotating roles** (measurer, recorder, checker, presenter) | modified | Peer-supported | Role chart | Mixed-ability, mixed-gender teams; roles rotate weekly so no one is always the "decorator" |
| 4 | Practise the concepts in class | unchanged | — | — | Concept teaching isn't reduced |
| 5 | **Each team asks one adult who uses this maths in daily work** (shopkeeper, farmer, tailor, conductor, a parent) one or two prepared questions | **added** | Connections across settings | Interview note | Done with a family member or on a teacher-accompanied walk |
| 6 | Weekly in-class support, **plus a 5-minute peer feedback round between teams** ("one thing that works, one question") | modified | Peer-supported | Feedback slips | — |
| 7 | Student project showcase **with parents or a younger class invited**; each team explains the maths, not only the result | modified | Production-centred / connections across settings | Team poster + spoken explanation | No photos of children shared outside school without written parental consent |

**Size check:** 7 tasks (≤9) · 1 added (≤2) · 1 month (≤8 weeks) ✔

### Success indicators
- Every team works on a problem it proposed (100% student-originated, teacher-approved).
- ≥80% of students can explain the concept used in their project at the showcase (teacher checklist).
- ≥1 family member per 3 students attends, or a younger class attends.

### Rationale (supportable claims only)
Connected Learning holds that learning is more engaging when it connects learners' own interests to academic goals, through peer collaboration and real audiences (reference cards: CL-interests, CL-peers, CL-connections-across-settings, CL-audience). The catalogue MIP already has projects and a showcase. This version moves the *choice of problem* to students and links the maths to adults who use it. **The effect on concept mastery is untested**; the success indicators measure it.
