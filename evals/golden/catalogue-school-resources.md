# Golden Example 6 — Catalogue MIP "School resources" (PB: prioritise APPLY; allocate CONDITIONAL; Connected Learning: ABSTAIN)

> **Role in the eval set:** the first example taken **directly from the MI catalogue** in its real format. It's the reference case for how participatory budgeting fits SMC practice: **prioritising needs with a ballot that informs the SMC's agreement**, a knapsack ballot **only if** a discretionary amount is confirmed, and resource **mobilisation left alone**.
> **Source:** MI catalogue (Commons #259), sheet *Access & Infra*, sub-category *Physical Infrastructure*, row 1, "School resources" (Mantra4Change & ShikshaLokam). Context: SMC Module 2 (Commons #595, Prajayatna).

## Expected gate decisions

| Gate | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| PB: prioritise | **APPLY** | The MIP has the HM list school needs, so there are competing needs. The SMC is the legitimate constituency, and RTE gives it the SDP role. |
| PB: allocate (knapsack) | **CONDITIONAL** | Applies only if the HM confirms a discretionary amount (e.g. untied grant headroom, SMC-raised funds). Otherwise skip. |
| PB: mobilise | **not PB** | Getting resources from the community, Gram Panchayat, block or donors is already in the MIP and the SMC module. Leave it as is. |
| Connected Learning | **ABSTAIN** | HM-owned administrative MIP; there's no youth learning design to improve. |

## Already present (credit before adding)

Needs assessment (task 1–2), community mobilisation (task 3), escalation to the cluster level (task 5). The gaps are that **nothing says how needs are prioritised** or by whom, and **nothing is reported back** to parents.

## Transformed MIP

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Title** | School resources: needs prioritised with the SMC |
| **Target stakeholder** | HM (unchanged), with the SMC as the deciding body |
| **Duration** | 3 weeks (+1 week for the SMC meeting) |
| **Objective** | *Unchanged:* improve school resources. *Added:* the priority order of needs is agreed by the SMC after a visible ballot, and published. |
| **Extra cost** | Nil (paper ballots) |

| # | Task | Change | Technique | Role / artefact | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Assess current resources and infrastructure | unchanged | — | — | — |
| 2 | Detail needs as a **numbered shortlist of 5–8**, each marked **immediate** (e.g. drinking water, cleanliness) or **long-term** (e.g. extra classroom), with one line of evidence | modified | — | Needs shortlist (chart) | Include needs of children with disabilities whether or not any are currently enrolled (per the SMC module) |
| 3 | **SMC priority meeting:** each SMC member and teacher (optionally 2 student representatives from Class 5+) ticks every need they consider most important on an **approval ballot**. The tally is shown on chart paper. The SMC then **discusses and agrees** the priority order. | **added** | PB: prioritise (approval voting) | Tally chart + agreed order in the SMC minutes | The ballot informs agreement; it doesn't override it. Women members vote and speak first; picture ballots where needed. |
| 4 | Identify resources the community, Gram Panchayat, block or donors can provide **for the agreed priorities** | modified | (mobilise; not PB) | — | Approach through the SMC or Gram Panchayat, not individually (per the SMC module) |
| 5 | List requirements to be procured for the agreed priorities. **If a discretionary amount is confirmed:** SMC members cast a **knapsack ballot** over costed items within that amount; count by the greedy rule (see Example 1). | modified | PB: allocate (conditional) | Funded list | Skip the ballot if there's no confirmed amount. Procurement stays with the HM/SMC under the usual rules. |
| 6 | Share the list with the CRP | unchanged | — | — | — |
| 7 | **Post a one-page summary** (priorities, tally, what's being sought from whom) on the notice board and read it at the next PTM | **added** | PB: report-back | Notice-board summary | No sensitive safety details |

**Size check:** 7 tasks (≤9) · 2 added (≤2) · 3 weeks (≤8) ✔

### Success indicators
- ≥60% of SMC members cast a ballot, including ≥50% of women members.
- The agreed priority order is recorded in the SMC minutes, and the notice is posted within one week.
- At least one priority need has a mobilisation request submitted (e.g. an application to the Gram Panchayat) by week 3.

### Rationale (supportable claims only)
The SMC module stresses collective decisions with equal voice for women and men, and asks whether grants are disclosed in meetings. A visible approval tally before discussion makes everyone's priorities known before the louder voices speak, and leaves a record. Approval voting is one of the methods on the Stanford PB platform. Whether this changes *which* needs get met is **untested**.
