# Golden Example 1 — Civics Lab MIP (revised)

> **Role in the eval set:** the reference case where the **participatory budgeting (PB) process** gate says **APPLY**, and Connected Learning also applies.
> Revised from the user-supplied "Civics Lab" outline (2026-11-04). The changes are listed at the end.

## Expected gate decisions

| Gate | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| PB process | **APPLY** | Students are a real constituency, the fund is a real shared resource, there are ≥2 competing options, and the outcome is public. |
| Budget-capped choice technique | APPLY (subsumed by the PB process) | There is a hard ₹ cap. |
| Connected Learning | **APPLY** | Production-centred (an artefact at every step), peer-supported (squads), shared purpose (a common school space), and connections across settings (SMC/parent report-back). |

## MIP metadata

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **MIP title** | School Commons: Students Decide How to Improve a Shared Space |
| **MIP owner** | Head Teacher (HT), with the School Management Committee (SMC) |
| **Student leads** | Student cabinet / club members (2 per participating class) |
| **Objective** | Students identify, cost, vote on and help deliver improvements to a shared school space or resource within a fixed fund, using a knapsack ballot. |
| **Domain (MI framework)** | Beneficiary Satisfaction; Management & Governance (secondary: Access & Infrastructure) |
| **Target group** | Standard: Classes 6–8 or 6–12. Primary variant: Classes 3–5 (see below). |
| **Constrained resource** | A fixed, SMC-approved amount, e.g. **₹10,000–₹20,000**. Possible sources: untied headroom in the composite school grant (after earmarked items), SMC-raised or community funds, or a CSR micro-grant. *The HT confirms the source and spending rules before Step 1.* |
| **Duration** | **Standard: 6 weeks** · **Lite: 2 weeks** (see Lite path) |
| **Success indicators** | (1) ≥70% of students in participating classes cast a valid ballot. (2) Funded projects are completed within 4 weeks of the vote. (3) Results and spending are published to students and the SMC. (4) Ballots were cast by at least as many girls, in proportion to enrolment. |

## Sequential steps

```
[1 Sense & Map] → [2 Cost & Check] → [3 Knapsack Vote] → [4 Co-Deliver] → [5 Report Back]
   Week 1–2          Week 2–3           Week 3              Week 4–6          Week 6
```

### Step 1 — Sense & Map (Weeks 1–2)
- **Action:** Discovery groups of 4–5 students (mixed by class, gender and section) walk the school. They photograph or sketch underused or broken spaces and ask 5 peers each what they would change.
- **Guardrail — evidence before ideas:** an issue enters the map only if it has a photo or sketch **and** at least 3 peer responses mentioning it.
- **Guardrail — safety:** a teacher accompanies every walk. Hazardous areas (electrical, water tanks, roof, construction) are reported, not entered.
- **Artefact:** *Student Need Map*, a chart-paper map of the school with numbered issues.

### Step 2 — Cost & Check (Weeks 2–3)
- **Action:** Each group turns one or two issues into a proposal. With a teacher or SMC member, they get a price from a local shop, a catalogue, or a past bill.
- **Guardrail — no price, no ballot:** every proposal needs an itemised cost. A proposal that can't be priced doesn't go on the ballot.
- **Guardrail — check feasibility, not merit:** the checking panel (HT + 1 SMC member + 2 student leads from different classes) may reject a proposal **only** if it (a) can't be priced, (b) is over the total cap, (c) breaks spending rules, or (d) is unsafe for students to help deliver. Every rejected proposal and its reason are posted on the notice board.
- **Artefact:** *Priced Proposal List*, with proposal number, one-line description, cost, and the student group.

### Step 3 — Knapsack Vote (Week 3) — the Stanford PB step
- **Ballot (knapsack ballot):** each voter gets the same budget, equal to the total fund. They tick any set of proposals whose costs add up to **no more than the cap**. Picking an expensive project means giving up cheaper ones.
- **Paper mechanics:** the ballot lists each proposal with its cost and a "running total" column. Before the ballot goes in the box, a student checker at the desk adds it up. If it's over the cap, it goes back to the voter to fix. *(Digital option: pbstanford.org enforces the cap automatically.)*
- **Counting rule (state it before voting):**
  1. Count how many ballots ticked each proposal.
  2. Sort proposals by vote count, highest first.
  3. Go down the list. Fund a proposal if it fits in the money left; otherwise skip it and try the next.
  4. Ties: fund the cheaper proposal first.
  *(This is the simple greedy rule. The Method of Equal Shares is fairer to minority preferences but impractical to tally by hand; use it only on a digital platform.)*
- **Guardrail — no popularity-only vote:** an over-cap ballot is invalid.
- **Guardrail — inclusion:** students who can't read the ballot get a picture ballot or a buddy reader. Every voter is told how the votes will be counted beforehand.
- **Artefact:** *Ballot Tally & Funded List*, posted publicly with vote counts and the running total spent.

### Step 4 — Co-Deliver (Weeks 4–6)
- **Action:** Each funded proposal gets a student squad and an adult owner.
  - **Adults (HT/SMC):** all buying, payments, receipts and vendor or physical work.
  - **Students:** specify what to buy, check delivered items against the proposal, and do *safe* tasks only: design, signage, painting murals at ground level, arranging, inventory, care routines.
- **Guardrail — no cash with students, no manual or sanitation labour by students.**
- **Artefact:** *Improvement Log*, with before/after photos, items received vs. proposed, and money spent vs. budgeted.

### Step 5 — Report Back (Week 6)
- **Action:** Student leads present the results at the morning assembly and at the next SMC/parents' meeting. They cover what was chosen, what it cost, and what was left unfunded (as candidates for next term).
- **Artefact:** *Public Results Board*, a notice-board display, kept up until the next cycle.

## Lite path (2 weeks)
Use this when time or capacity is short.
1. **Day 1–3:** each class lists up to 3 issues in one class period (no school walk).
2. **Day 4–7:** the HT and 2 student leads price the top issues (5–8 proposals in total).
3. **Day 8:** one class period for the knapsack ballot, counted the same day.
4. **Day 9–14:** adults buy, students check, results are posted.

## Primary variant (Classes 3–5)
- Picture cards instead of a written ballot. The budget is shown as tokens or coins (e.g. 20 tokens = the fund), and each proposal is priced in tokens.
- Children place tokens in proposal jars. The teacher checks that no child uses more than their tokens.
- Count tokens per jar, then fund from the top as in Step 3.

## What this MIP is *not*
- Not a way to raise money from students or parents.
- Not a substitute for repairs the department is responsible for (structural, electrical, water).
- Not a popularity contest: the cap and the trade-offs are the point.

## Changes from the original outline

| Original | Revised | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "PTA micro-grant", "$250" | SMC-approved fund, ₹ range, source confirmed by the HT | Fits Indian government school finance and spending rules |
| Students "purchase items and build" | Adults buy and do physical work; students specify, check, and do safe tasks | Safeguarding; no cash with minors; child-labour optics |
| "Peer selection board" judges pitches | Checking panel can reject only for feasibility, price, rules or safety; rejections are published | Prevents filtering out ideas from quieter or marginalised students |
| "Knapsack Voting or Equal Shares Allocator" | Knapsack ballot + an explicit greedy counting rule; Equal Shares only if digital | Ballot format and counting rule are different things; Equal Shares isn't a Stanford method |
| No duration | 6-week standard, 2-week Lite path | Keeps it "micro" |
| Grades 6–12 only | Adds a primary token-and-jar variant | Many government schools are primary-only |
| 4 steps | Adds Step 5, Report Back | Makes the decision public and accountable (shared purpose, connections across settings) |
| No owner or indicators | HT/SMC owner, measurable success indicators | MIP template expectations |
