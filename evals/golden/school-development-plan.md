# Golden Example 5 — School Development Plan (Connected Learning: ABSTAIN; participatory process: APPLY)

> **Role in the eval set:** the reference case where the **Connected Learning label must not be applied**, because this is an HM/SMC governance plan, not a youth learning design. A participatory prioritisation step, using Stanford PB voting methods, **does** apply. It's the mirror image of Time-Knapsack.
> **Source:** Shikshagraha Commons resource **#751**, "School Development Plan" (Vidhya Vidhai Foundation, PDF, 14 pp.). The "before" is a structured summary. Load the original from `evals/sources.md` for evals.

## Expected gate decisions

| Gate | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Connected Learning | **ABSTAIN** | The learner here is the institution and its leadership, not young people's learning. Adding CL vocabulary would be framework-washing. |
| PB process | **APPLY (partial)** | The SMC is a constituency with a statutory role. The plan must choose among competing needs. Where the drafted actions carry ₹ costs, there's a shared resource. |
| Budget-capped choice | APPLY (subsumed by PB) | Only for costed actions. Prioritising *challenges* uses approval or ranked voting (no costs needed). |

## Before: what the source actually says (summary)

- **Purpose:** help the HM be strategic: prioritise and identify actions that improve teaching and learning.
- **HM role:** set the vision; increase community participation and ownership; include stakeholders in planning and decisions; create opportunities for student engagement in school processes.
- **SDP journey:** meeting or survey with **teachers, SMC members and students** → draft the SDP using their inputs → **review meeting with teachers, SMC and students to finalise** → assign an in-charge and timeline for each intervention → quarterly monitoring → monthly progress updates to teachers and SMC.
- **Roles:** teachers identify challenges and carry out actions; the **SMC is involved in drafting** and monitors use of grants; **students take part in review** and share perspectives through conversations or surveys.
- **SMC under RTE 2009:** monitor the school, prepare and recommend the School Development Programme, assess financial requirements, and monitor grant use.
- **Process:**
  1. **Vision & mission** (guiding questions with a worked example).
  2. **Needs analysis** checklist: enrolment and RTE norms, learning outcomes, leadership and management, stakeholder involvement, teaching–learning practices, resources.
  3. **Drafting table:** *Challenge · Goal (SMART) · Solution · Steps/Sub-tasks · Timeline · Who · Resources needed · Monitoring tools/data*.
  4. **Self-review checklist** (vision set, needs analysed, teachers, SMC and students included).

**Correction to the earlier paraphrase:** the source isn't top-down with "compliant feedback". It already includes teachers, the SMC and students, cites the SMC's RTE role, and has a review meeting to finalise the plan.

**Note for the schema:** the Step 3 drafting table is effectively an MIP structure (challenge → SMART goal → solution → tasks → owner → timeline → resources → monitoring). It's a strong candidate for `schema/mip.schema.json` until the official MIP template is confirmed.

## Participatory reading of the source

| Aspect | Present | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Who is consulted | Teachers, SMC, students | Parents are only *informed*; students are consulted and review, but **don't decide** |
| How priorities are chosen | Needs analysis → HM drafts | **No transparent prioritisation rule** when needs compete; the HM effectively chooses |
| Resources | Asks how often resource needs are reviewed | **No allocation step** linking costed actions to available funds |
| Accountability | Quarterly monitoring, monthly updates to teachers and SMC | No public report-back to students and parents |

## After: revised MIP (adds a prioritisation step between Step 2 and Step 3)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **MIP title** | Choosing Our School's Priorities Together: an SMC-led prioritisation step for the SDP |
| **MIP owner** | HM, with the **SMC as convening and adopting body** (per RTE) |
| **Objective** | The SDP's top priorities and its costed actions are chosen through a transparent SMC-convened vote with student and parent participation. Results are published. |
| **Domain** | Management & Governance |
| **Duration** | 3 weeks, inserted into the source's existing SDP cycle |
| **Extra cost** | Nil (paper ballots, one SMC meeting, one assembly period) |

### Steps

1. **Shortlist challenges (week 1):** from the source's Step 2 needs analysis, the HM and SMC list **5–8 candidate challenges** in plain language. Each has one line of evidence from the analysis.
2. **Priority vote (week 1–2):** an **approval ballot** (tick every challenge you think matters most). It runs in three groups, tallied separately and then together:
   - SMC members (which by norm include parents);
   - a student group (class representatives or cabinet, Classes 5+);
   - teachers.

   The SMC adopts the top **2–3 challenges**. *Approval voting is one of the methods on the Stanford PB platform. It's used here because challenges don't have costs yet.*
3. **Draft and cost (week 2):** for the chosen challenges, teachers complete the source's **Step 3 drafting table**, including the *Resources needed* column with ₹ estimates.
4. **Knapsack ballot for funded actions (week 3), only if there is discretionary money** (e.g. untied grant headroom or SMC-raised funds): SMC members cast a **knapsack ballot** within the available amount. Count with the same greedy rule as Golden Example 1 (Civics Lab). If there's no discretionary money, skip this step.
5. **Adopt and publish (week 3):** the SMC adopts the SDP at its meeting. A **one-page public summary** (priorities, vote tallies, funded actions, owners, timeline) goes on the notice board and is read at assembly.
6. **Report back (quarterly):** the source's quarterly monitoring is also shared at an SMC/parents' meeting and with student representatives.

### Guardrails
- **SMC is the decision body:** votes advise the SMC; the SMC adopts the plan (RTE).
- **Redaction:** the public summary leaves out safety vulnerabilities, staff performance issues and any child-level data.
- **Inclusion:** picture or oral ballots where needed. Parent ballots are available at pickup times, not just at meetings. Track participation by gender.
- **No new tools assumed:** paper ballots and the notice board. A digital option (e.g. pbstanford.org) is optional.
- **No Connected Learning relabelling:** the student group's participation is civic participation, not a "learning design". If the school wants a youth-learning angle, that's a separate MIP (e.g. a student cabinet running the ballot as a civics project).

### Success indicators
- The SDP's adopted priorities match the published tally (transparency).
- ≥60% of SMC members, ≥70% of student representatives and all teachers cast ballots.
- The public summary is posted within 1 week of adoption, and quarterly report-backs happen.
