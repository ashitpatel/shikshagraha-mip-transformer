# Connected Learning — Reference Cards

> **Purpose:** the transformer and critic prompts read this file. Each card gives the **gate test**, the **edits allowed** to an MIP, **anti-patterns** the critic rejects, and the **claims a rationale may make**. Any rationale claim must cite a card ID (`basis: CL-…`) and stay within that card's *Supportable claims*. A claim that isn't listed there, or any number, is rejected.

## What the framework is, in one paragraph

Connected Learning describes learning that is **interest-driven, supported by relationships, and connected to opportunity**: academic, economic or civic [S1]. The 2013 agenda organised it as three spheres (**interests, peer culture, academic orientation**) and three design features (**production-centred, shared purpose, openly networked**). The 2020 review recast the spheres as **interests, relationships, opportunities**, and the qualities of supportive experiences as **sponsorship of youth interests, shared practices, shared purpose, connections across settings** [S2]. It's an **equity agenda** as much as a learning one: it was motivated by the observation that interest-driven learning opportunities tend to reach already-advantaged young people [S1, S2].

## Nature of the evidence (governs every card)

- The evidence base is mainly **ethnographic, qualitative and design-based research**, plus some survey studies. It describes *how* and *when* engagement and learning happen. It doesn't establish effect sizes on test scores.
- **Never claim** a numerical effect, "proven", "research shows X% / N-times", or a causal effect on learning outcomes from Connected Learning.
- Research is mostly from the US and UK, often about out-of-school and digital settings. **Applying it to Indian government schools is an extrapolation**; say so if asked.

## Scope card

### CL-scope — when Connected Learning applies at all

- **Gate test:** is there a **young person's learning experience** inside the MIP that the MIP owner can shape?
  - **APPLY:** teacher-owned pedagogy MIPs; student-facing clubs, reading and projects; Learning Centre activities.
  - **PARTIAL:** HM- or SMC-owned MIPs where students take part in a task that can be designed as a learning experience (e.g. a student cabinet running a survey).
  - **ABSTAIN:** administrative, HR, infrastructure, or governance MIPs with no student learning experience (e.g. staff meetings, teacher motivation, procurement, SDP drafting).
- **Anti-patterns:** applying it to teachers as learners and calling it Connected Learning (a defensible *extension*, but it must be labelled as such); relabelling community participation as Connected Learning (that's participatory governance; see `PB-scope`).

## Principle cards

### CL-interests — interest-powered learning
- **Means:** learning activity draws on what the learner already cares about or wants to explore [S1]. In 2020 terms, adults **sponsor** those interests [S2].
- **Gate test:** does the MIP give students *any* choice of topic, theme, problem or format?
- **Allowed edits:** add a student-choice step *within* an existing task (pick a theme, propose a problem within teacher-set frames); add 5–10 theme words or examples drawn from student interests.
- **Anti-patterns:** replacing the curricular or foundational objective with a free-choice activity; interest "choices" that are all teacher-picked; assuming interests are digital or urban.
- **Supportable claims:** "Connected Learning research describes engagement as stronger when learning connects to learners' own interests." "The framework treats adult support for youth interests as a key condition."

### CL-peers — peer-supported learning
- **Means:** young people learn through sharing, feedback and collaboration with peers [S1].
- **Gate test:** are students only recipients, or do they teach, review or collaborate with each other?
- **Allowed edits:** structured peer roles (rotating), peer-led circles, short peer-feedback rounds, students as teachers.
- **Anti-patterns:** fixed roles that reproduce hierarchy (same student always leads; girls always decorate); peer teaching used to replace teacher instruction.
- **Supportable claims:** "Connected Learning research highlights peer sharing and feedback as a central way young people learn."

### CL-relationships — relationships with caring adults and mentors
- **Means:** the 2020 framing widens "peer culture" to **relationships**, including adults (mentors, family, community members) who recognise and support young people's interests [S2].
- **Gate test:** does any adult besides the teacher connect with students' learning in this MIP?
- **Allowed edits:** a short, structured contact with a community or family adult tied to the activity (interview, demonstration, audience).
- **Anti-patterns:** unsupervised adult–child contact; inviting only "distinguished" professionals (reinforces whose knowledge counts); steering children toward caste- or gender-associated trades.
- **Supportable claims:** "The framework identifies relationships with supportive adults, beyond teachers, as one of three core spheres of connected learning."

### CL-opportunities — connection to academic, economic or civic opportunity
- **Means:** interest-driven activity is linked to recognised opportunities: academic credit, skills, careers, civic roles [S1, S2]. (It was "academically oriented" in 2013.)
- **Gate test:** is the activity tied to a curriculum outcome, assessment or future pathway?
- **Allowed edits:** state the syllabus outcome the activity serves; link to career awareness (e.g. Dream Job Map); keep mastery checks.
- **Anti-patterns:** interest activity with no academic link; dropping assessment.
- **Supportable claims:** "Connected Learning emphasises linking interests to recognised academic or career opportunities, so engagement translates into progress."

### CL-production — production-centred / shared practices
- **Means:** learners make things (artefacts, performances, media, solutions), often through shared practices with others [S1, S2].
- **Gate test:** does the MIP end in something students make, or only in completion of tasks?
- **Allowed edits:** turn evidence of completion into a student-made artefact (poster, mini-book, model, oral presentation).
- **Anti-patterns:** requiring devices or paid materials; production that crowds out practice time on core skills.
- **Supportable claims:** "The framework treats making and sharing things as a central learning practice."

### CL-shared-purpose — shared purpose
- **Means:** young people and adults work toward common goals, across ages and roles [S1, S2].
- **Gate test:** is there a goal the group owns together, rather than only individual or competing goals?
- **Allowed edits:** collective targets (e.g. a whole-grade bonus alongside house competition); co-planning steps with students.
- **Anti-patterns:** pure competition with no shared stake; "co-design" that's consultation in name only.
- **Supportable claims:** "Connected Learning describes shared goals across peers and adults as a feature of supportive learning environments."

### CL-connections-across-settings — connections across settings
- **Means:** learning links home, community and school, so that what a young person knows or does in one setting is recognised in another [S2]. (This replaces "openly networked" from 2013; the original emphasis on digital networks is **not required**.)
- **Gate test:** is anything from students' homes or neighbourhoods used, or is work shown beyond the classroom?
- **Allowed edits:** home or neighbourhood problems as project content; family or community audiences; interviews with community members.
- **Anti-patterns:** equating this with going online; collecting sensitive household data (income, caste); publishing children's images without consent.
- **Supportable claims:** "The 2020 Connected Learning framing emphasises connecting learning across home, community and school settings."

### CL-audience — real audiences (a project shorthand)
- **Means:** work is shared with people who care about it: younger students, families, community. This is a *project shorthand* combining production [S1] with connections across settings [S2]; **it isn't a named principle in the sources**, so cite it together with `CL-production` or `CL-connections-across-settings`.
- **Gate test:** who sees the students' work besides the teacher?
- **Allowed edits:** poster walks, melas, PTM showcases, presenting to a younger class.
- **Anti-patterns:** default online publication; showcases only top students present at.
- **Supportable claims:** "Sharing work with audiences who care about it is part of how the framework describes production and cross-setting connection."

## Ecosystem and pathway cards (Pinkard extension)

These extend `CL-connections-across-settings`, `CL-relationships` and `CL-opportunities` with design ideas from Pinkard's work [S4, S5]. Pinkard co-authored [S2], so they sit within Connected Learning, not beside it. All three are subject to **CL-G9: map assets, never children**.

### CL-asset-map — community learning-asset map
- **Means:** identify the learning opportunities around a school (places, programmes, people) so school activities can link to them, and gaps in access become visible. This adapts the ecosystem mapping in [S5].
- **Gate test:** does the MIP connect students to anything outside school, and would a shared, visible list of local opportunities make that connection concrete?
- **Allowed edits:** a chart-paper or wall map compiled by students and teachers, listing **places** (panchayat or public library, playground, NGO or community learning centre, health centre), **programmes** (free classes, clubs, melas), and **people willing to help** (craftspeople, shopkeepers, health workers, retired teachers), with consent from those listed. Reuse it across MIPs. In SMC contexts the same map supports resource mobilisation (`PB-not-pb`).
- **Already present in:** the Play Book's Neighbourhood Explorer and Learning Wall (Commons #753); SMC Module 2's session on identifying resources (Commons #595). **Credit these first; don't duplicate.**
- **Anti-patterns:** recording which children attend what; an online-only or app-based map; listing adults without their consent; listing paid coaching as a "learning asset" without noting cost.
- **Supportable claims:** "Pinkard and colleagues mapped out-of-school learning opportunities in Chicago to see where opportunities exist and where access is uneven." [S5]

### CL-brokering — adults who connect a student to the next opportunity
- **Means:** a teacher, mentor or family member deliberately connects a young person to a specific next opportunity related to their interest. This is the "pathway" idea in DYN's design [S4], and it fits the 2020 emphasis on adult **sponsorship** of youth interests [S2].
- **Gate test:** after the MIP's activity, does anything help a student take a next step on their interest beyond this classroom?
- **Allowed edits:** one task in which the teacher matches each student (or interest circle) to **one** concrete next opportunity from the asset map, and tells the student and a parent in person, by note or by phone. Optionally, one follow-up check two to four weeks later.
- **Anti-patterns:** generic advice ("explore your interests"); steering students toward options by caste, gender or family occupation; referrals to paid services; automated messages from unverified tools.
- **Supportable claims:** "DYN combined in-school and out-of-school programmes with mentors, so that students' media work could continue across settings." [S4] "The 2020 framing treats adult sponsorship of young people's interests as a key condition." [S2]

### CL-transitions — attention to school transitions
- **Means:** students are most likely to lose connections at points of transition (in DYN's case, middle school [S4]). In Indian government schools the comparable points are **Class 5→6 and Class 8→9**, often a change of school, where dropout risk rises.
- **Gate test:** is the MIP aimed at, or does it include, students in a transition year?
- **Allowed edits:** a short "next school, next step" task: a visit to or from the receiving school, or a peer from the next stage; carrying one interest artefact or a short note across the transition; telling families about the asset-map opportunities near the next school.
- **Anti-patterns:** sharing student records between schools beyond official procedures; labelling individual students as "at risk" in anything students or other families can see.
- **Supportable claims:** "DYN focused on middle-school students and on continuity across school, community, home and online settings." [S4] *(Don't claim effects on dropout; label as untested.)*

## Cross-cutting guardrails (critic checks on every CL transformation)

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| CL-G1 **Keep the objective** | The MIP's objective and core method stay. In foundational literacy and numeracy, the structured sequence and mastery checks are never replaced. |
| CL-G2 **Credit first** | List what the MIP already does for each principle before proposing additions. |
| CL-G3 **Change, not vocabulary** | Every change must alter a task, role, artefact or audience. Wording-only changes are rejected. |
| CL-G4 **Offline by default** | No device, connectivity or paid material is required. Digital options are optional. |
| CL-G5 **Child safeguarding** | Supervised adult contact only. No children's images, voices or names online without written parental consent (India DPDP Act 2023). No sensitive household data. |
| CL-G6 **Equity** | Choices open to all students, not just leaders. Mixed groups, rotating roles. No caste or gender steering. |
| CL-G7 **Micro limits** | ≤8 weeks, ≤9 tasks, ≤2 added tasks, no new paid inputs (see the schema `x_limits`). |
| CL-G8 **Evidence honesty** | Rationale claims come only from the *Supportable claims* above. Effects on outcomes are stated as untested. |
| CL-G9 **Map assets, never children** | No collection, tracking or analysis of individual students' out-of-school activity (library visits, group participation, home device use, location), whether by staff, a platform or an AI tool. Maps list places, programmes and consenting adults only. Any aggregate figure must be anonymous and gathered with consent. (India DPDP Act 2023: verifiable parental consent for children's data.) |
