# Walkthrough 1 — Students decide how to improve a shared school space

> A **reference answer** written by hand, not output from the tool (the pipeline hasn't been run live yet). The starting plan was **authored for this example** (a "seed"), because the Civics Lab was proposed as a new MIP. Technical version: [evals/golden/civics-lab.md](../../evals/golden/civics-lab.md).

## 1. The starting point
A school has a **confirmed, unearmarked fund** (say ₹20,000 this term) to improve a space students use every day. In the starting plan, the HM briefs the student cabinet, the cabinet lists problems, the **HM gets quotes and decides alone** what to fund, items are bought, and the cabinet looks after the space.

## 2. What it already does well
- Students are involved: the cabinet lists problems and looks after the space.
- There's a real, confirmed amount of money.

**The gap:** the actual decision (what to spend on) is made by one person, and nobody beyond the cabinet has a say or sees why.

## 3. What was applied, and why

| Method | In plain words | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| **Collecting ideas with evidence** | Small mixed groups walk the school, photograph or sketch problems, ask peers | An issue only goes on the map if it has a photo or sketch and 3 peer mentions. That keeps the choice grounded in what students actually see. |
| **Costing check** | Each proposal gets a real price before the vote | "No price, no ballot." It stops wish-lists and makes the trade-offs real. |
| **Knapsack ballot** (from the Stanford participatory budgeting work) | Every voter ticks projects whose costs add up to **no more than the fund** | The fund is real and options exceed it. Voters have to weigh "one big thing or three small ones" themselves. |
| **Counting rule announced first** | Count ticks per project; fund from the top while money lasts; skip what doesn't fit | Simple enough to count by hand in public, and announced in advance so nobody can change it after the count |
| **Report-back** | Results and spending shown at assembly and the SMC/parents' meeting | Makes the decision accountable, and unfunded ideas become candidates for next term |
| **Shared purpose, production, peer squads** (Connected Learning) | Students make the Need Map, the proposal list and the Improvement Log, working in squads | Students make real artefacts for a goal the whole school shares |

## 4. What was deliberately not done, and why
- **Students don't handle money or do building work.** Adults buy and pay; students specify, check deliveries and do safe tasks (design, signage, arranging).
- **The checking panel can't judge ideas on merit.** It may reject only for: no price, over the cap, against spending rules, or unsafe. Every rejection is posted. This stops quieter students' ideas being filtered out.
- **No "Equal Shares" counting on paper.** It's fairer to minority preferences, but impractical to tally by hand; it's an option only on a digital platform.
- **No US framing:** "PTA micro-grant" and "$250" were replaced by an SMC-approved fund in rupees.

## 5. Before → after

| Before (starting plan) | After |
|---|---|
| HM briefs the cabinet | **Weeks 1–2: Sense & Map.** Mixed groups walk the school (teacher present) and build a Need Map |
| Cabinet lists problems | **Weeks 2–3: Cost & Check.** Groups turn issues into priced proposals; the panel checks feasibility only and posts rejections |
| HM gets quotes and decides | **Week 3: Knapsack vote.** Every student votes within the fund; counted publicly by the announced rule |
| Procure and complete | **Weeks 4–6: Co-deliver.** Adults buy and do physical work; student squads specify, check and do safe tasks |
| Cabinet looks after the space | **Week 6: Report back** at assembly and the SMC meeting, plus the existing care rota |

**Lite path (2 weeks):** each class lists 3 issues in one period → the HM and 2 student leads price 5–8 proposals → one-period ballot, counted the same day → adults buy, students check, results posted.
**Primary variant (Classes 3–5):** picture cards; the fund shown as tokens; children put tokens in proposal jars.

## 6. Safety and fairness rules
Teacher accompanies every walk · hazardous areas reported, not entered · no cash with students · no manual or sanitation labour by students · picture ballot or buddy reader for students who can't read the ballot · participation tracked by gender.

## 7. Teacher card

> # School Commons: students decide how to improve a shared space
> **Who runs it:** HM, with the SMC · **Time:** 6 weeks (lite: 2) · **Cost:** the approved fund only
> **Goal:** Students identify, cost, vote on and help deliver improvements to a shared space within the fund.
>
> ## Steps
> 1. Mixed student groups map problems in shared spaces, with photos and peer comments. *Remember:* a teacher goes on every walk.
> 2. Groups turn issues into priced proposals; a small panel checks only price, rules and safety. *Remember:* post every rejection and its reason.
> 3. Every student votes, choosing proposals that fit within the fund. Count in public using the rule announced beforehand.
> 4. Adults buy and do physical work; student squads check deliveries and do safe tasks. *Remember:* no cash with students.
> 5. Students present the results and spending at assembly and the SMC meeting.
>
> ## How you'll know it worked
> - At least 70% of students cast a valid ballot
> - Funded projects finished within 4 weeks of the vote
> - Results and spending posted for students and the SMC
>
> ## Before you start
> - Confirm the fund amount and the spending rules with the SMC.
>
> ## What changed from the original (and why)
> | Step | Change | Why |
> |---|---|---|
> | 1–2 | Students gather evidence and price ideas | So choices rest on real needs and real costs |
> | 3 | Whole-school vote within the fund | Students weigh the trade-offs themselves, instead of one person deciding |
> | 5 | Public report-back | So the decision is accountable and unfunded ideas carry forward |
>

## 8. What to watch for
Whether the walk and costing fit in the time (use the lite path if not) · whether younger or quieter students' ideas survive the panel · whether the SMC is comfortable letting a student vote shape the spend.
