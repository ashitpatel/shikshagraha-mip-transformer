# Walkthrough 11 — "Letters and Words" becomes a daily letters-and-sounds block

> A **reference answer** written by hand, not tool output. Source: ShikshaLokam's *Domain-wise Micro Improvements Framework* (Commons #259), "Letters and Words" (English/FLN teacher, 1 month), a real catalogue MIP. The context (Class 1–2, few devices) is assumed. The same structure applies to akshara and matras in the regional-language period. ## 1. The starting point
Find phonics activities in the Read-Along app → make a one-month plan → small groups → a group score chart → run the activities → total the scores → reflect.

## 2. What it already does well
It **focuses on letters and sounds**, has a **plan for the month**, uses **small groups**, and tracks **group progress**.
**The gaps:** it depends on a device many classrooms don't have; it isn't **daily** or clearly **sequenced**; children don't move on to **reading words and sentences**; and a total score doesn't show which children moved.

## 3. What was applied, and why

| Method | In plain words | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral check before and after** | Each child names letter-sounds and reads 5 simple words, about a minute each, at the start and the end | Shows each child's starting point and progress |
| **Daily explicit sound-symbol block** | 15–20 minutes every day at a fixed time: 3–4 new sounds a week → blend sounds → read words → read a short sentence together; slates for writing sounds; a quick oral check of a few children every Friday | Explicit sound-and-decoding teaching is rated as having strong evidence for the early grades. In a 2025 World Bank review of about 120 studies in low- and middle-income countries, all eight high-performing large reading programmes used it. *A one-month school project should expect smaller gains than these programmes, and this school's result is untested.* |

## 4. What was deliberately not done, and why
- **The app isn't required.** The daily block works on slates and cards; the Read-Along app adds practice where devices exist.
- **Not drills alone.** Every day ends with reading real words and a short sentence; sounds are learned in order to read.
- **The groups and the group score chart stay** (scores are for groups, not named children), with rotating roles (reader, pointer, checker).
- **Nothing in the existing language lessons is removed.** The block is added to the language period.

## 5. Before → after

8 tasks · 1 added · 1 month.

## 6. Safety and fairness rules
Individual results stay on the teacher's sheet · group scores only on the wall · groups mixed by gender · no child is left at zero words without a plan for extra help (e.g. in-school tutoring).

## 7. Teacher card

> # Letters and Sounds, every day
> **Who runs it:** English/FLN teacher · **Time:** 1 month · **Cost:** nil
> **Goal:** Teach letters and words with the Read-Along app. *Added:* a daily letters-and-sounds block so every child learns, with or without a device.
>
> ## Steps
> 1. Ask each child to say some letter-sounds and read 5 simple words. Note it on your sheet.
> 2. Plan the month: 3–4 new sounds a week, then blending, then words, then short sentences.
> 3. Make small groups with rotating roles (reader, pointer, checker).
> 4. Keep the group score chart (group scores only).
> 5. Every day, at the same time, for 15–20 minutes: new sounds → blend → read words → read a short sentence together. Use the app for extra practice if you have devices.
> 6. Every Friday, listen to 5–6 children read for a minute (take turns through the class).
> 7. At the end of the month, repeat step 1 and compare.
> 8. Reflect on what worked and who needs more help.
>
> ## How you'll know it worked
> - The block happened on at least 80% of school days
> - Every child reads more sounds and words at the end than at the start
>
> ## What changed from the original (and why)
> | Step | Change | Why |
> |---|---|---|
> | 2, 5 | A daily, step-by-step block | Explicit, daily sound-and-reading practice has strong evidence |
> | 5 | App optional | So every child learns, with or without a device |
> | 1, 6, 7 | Simple reading checks | So you can see who's moving and who needs help |
>

## 8. What to watch for
Whether the block really happens daily · whether children start reading short sentences by week 3 · whether some children stay at zero, in which case add tutoring (walkthrough 10).
