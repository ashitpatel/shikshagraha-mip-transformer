# Walkthrough 5 — Choosing the school's priorities together (School Development Plan)

> A **reference answer** written by hand, not tool output. Source: Shikshagraha Commons resource **#751**, *School Development Plan* (Vidhya Vidhai Foundation). Technical version: [evals/golden/school-development-plan.md](../../evals/golden/school-development-plan.md).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Runs it** | HM, with the **SMC as the deciding body** |
| **Time** | 3 weeks, inserted into the existing SDP cycle |
| **Cost** | Nil (paper ballots, one SMC meeting, one assembly period) |
| **Methods applied** | Participatory budgeting techniques: **priority vote** (approval ballot), **knapsack ballot only if money is confirmed**, **public report-back** |
| **Ruled out** | **Connected Learning**: this is school governance, not a student learning design. Also design workshops with officials, AI-generated surveys, and publishing the full plan. |

## 1. The starting point
The source guides an HM through drafting a School Development Plan (SDP):
1. Meet or survey teachers, SMC members and students about needs
2. Set the vision and mission
3. Analyse needs (enrolment, learning outcomes, leadership, involvement, teaching practice, resources)
4. Draft the plan: challenge → SMART goal → solution → steps → timeline → owner → resources → monitoring
5. Review meeting with teachers, SMC and students to finalise it
6. Assign owners; monitor quarterly; update teachers and SMC monthly

## 2. What it already does well
It already involves **teachers, the SMC and students**, cites the **SMC's role under the RTE Act**, has a review meeting, and has a clear drafting table.
**Correction:** an earlier proposal described the SDP as "top-down compliance". It isn't.

**The gaps:** when needs compete, **nothing says how priorities are chosen**; the HM effectively decides. Parents are only informed, students only consulted. Costed actions aren't linked to available money. Nothing is reported back to parents and students.

## 3. What was applied, and why

| Method | In plain words | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| **Priority vote** (approval ballot, one of the Stanford PB platform's methods) | SMC members, a student group and teachers each tick every challenge they think matters most; tallies shown separately and together | Needs compete, and the SMC is the legitimate body. A visible tally shows everyone's priorities, including women's and quieter members', before discussion starts. |
| **Knapsack ballot (only with confirmed money)** | If there is discretionary money, SMC members choose costed actions within that amount | Only real, unearmarked money can be allocated this way; otherwise the step is skipped |
| **Report-back** | A one-page public summary: priorities, tallies, funded actions, owners, timeline. Quarterly progress shared with parents and students. | Makes the plan accountable to the people it affects |

## 4. What was deliberately not done, and why
- **Not labelled Connected Learning.** Connected Learning is about how young people learn. A school's planning process is governance. (If a school wants a learning angle, a student cabinet running the ballot as a civics project would be a *separate* MIP.)
- **The vote doesn't override the SMC.** SMC practice is to decide by agreement, and the ballot is an input to that discussion. The SMC adopts the plan, as the RTE Act requires.
- **No design workshops with officials** (too large for a micro-improvement), no "AI Saathi" surveys (the tool couldn't be verified), and the **full plan isn't published**: only a summary, without safety weaknesses, staff issues or child data.
- **No tracking of children's out-of-school activity** (proposed in a later overhaul). Community resources can be mapped as places and programmes, never as data about individual children.

## 5. Before → after (a step inserted between "analyse needs" and "draft")

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Analyse needs | Same, then **shortlist 5–8 challenges in plain language, each with one line of evidence** |
| — | **Priority vote:** approval ballots in three groups (SMC, student representatives from Class 5+, teachers); tallies posted; the **SMC agrees the top 2–3** |
| Draft the plan | Same drafting table, **only for the agreed challenges**, with ₹ estimates in "resources needed" |
| — | **If discretionary money is confirmed:** SMC knapsack ballot over costed actions, counted by the announced rule. *Otherwise skip.* |
| Review meeting to finalise | **SMC adopts the plan; one-page public summary** on the notice board and read at assembly |
| Quarterly monitoring | Same, **shared at an SMC/parents' meeting and with student representatives** |

## 6. Safety and fairness rules
Picture or spoken ballots where needed · parent ballots available at pickup times, not only at meetings · participation tracked by gender · the public summary is redacted · student voice labelled as an addition to usual SMC practice.

## 7. Teacher card (for the HM)

> # Choosing our school's priorities together
> **Who runs it:** HM, with the SMC deciding · **Time:** 3 weeks within the SDP cycle · **Cost:** nil
> **Goal:** The SDP's top priorities and costed actions are chosen through a visible, SMC-led vote with student and parent voices, and the results are published.
>
> ## Steps
> 1. From your needs analysis, list 5–8 challenges, each with one line of evidence.
> 2. Run approval ballots for SMC members, student representatives and teachers; post the tallies.
> 3. At the SMC meeting, agree the top 2–3 priorities. *Remember:* the tally informs the discussion; the SMC decides.
> 4. Teachers complete the drafting table for those priorities, with cost estimates.
> 5. *Only if spare money is confirmed:* SMC members choose costed actions within that amount.
> 6. The SMC adopts the plan; post a one-page summary and read it at assembly. *Remember:* leave out safety weaknesses, staff issues and child data.
> 7. Share progress each quarter with parents and student representatives.
>
> ## How you'll know it worked
> - Adopted priorities match the published tally
> - At least 60% of SMC members and 70% of student representatives vote
> - Summary posted within a week; quarterly report-backs held
>
> ## Before you start
> - Is any money discretionary (not earmarked)? If not, skip step 5.
>
> ## What changed from the original (and why)
> | Step | Change | Why |
> |---|---|---|
> | 1–3 | Visible priority vote before drafting | So priorities reflect everyone, not one person |
> | 5 | Budget vote only with confirmed money | So nothing is promised that can't be funded |
> | 6–7 | Public summary and report-backs | So the plan is accountable to families and students |
>

## 8. What to watch for
Whether parents actually vote (timing matters) · whether the SMC treats the tally as input rather than as a verdict · whether the redacted summary stays useful.
