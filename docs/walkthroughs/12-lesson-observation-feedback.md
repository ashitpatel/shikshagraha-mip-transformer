# Walkthrough 12 — Weekly lesson observation and feedback on the state lesson guides

## 1. The starting point
Observe the school → write ideas on non-violent communication and active listening → discuss students' performance with one teacher → note feedback → note feedback techniques → give feedback to more teachers → reflect on DIKSHA.

## 2. What it already does well
It already **observes and gives one-to-one feedback**, and it pays attention to **how** feedback is given (non-violent communication, active listening). That matters, because the evidence-based version must be supportive.
**The gaps:** the observation isn't tied to **what the teacher is supposed to be teaching that day**, there's no short **checklist**, feedback is about general "performance" rather than the **lesson**, and it happens **once** rather than weekly.

## 3. What was applied, and why

| Method | In plain words | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| **Feedback on the state lesson guides** | Make a 5-item checklist from the NIPUN guide; each week, watch one lesson per Class 1–3 teacher, matched to that day's guide lesson; note one strength and one specific next step; talk for 10 minutes the same day; repeat for 3 weeks | Structured pedagogy (lesson guides, materials, coaching) is rated a "Great Buy", averaging about 0.23 SD in language. On-site coaching on structured lessons raised reading by about 0.23 SD in South African public schools. *A head teacher's weekly loop is a lighter version of the coaching studied, so expect a smaller effect; this school's result is untested.* |

## 4. What was deliberately not done, and why
- **Not an inspection.** No scores, no unannounced visits, nothing sent for appraisal. Feedback is about the lesson, and the tracking sheet is shared with the teacher, not posted.
- **The non-violent communication preparation stays.** It's how the feedback is delivered.
- **The DIKSHA reflection stays** as the original platform requires.
- **Only where the guides are in use.** Without them, "observe against the guide" has nothing to observe; the tool asks first.

## 6. Safety and fairness rules
Supportive, not evaluative · no use for appraisal · the tracking sheet is shared only with the teacher · feedback is about the lesson, never the person.

## 7. Teacher card (for the HM)

> # Weekly lesson observation and feedback on the NIPUN guides
> **Who runs it:** HM · **Time:** 3 weeks · **Cost:** nil
> **Goal:** Observe and give feedback to teachers. *Added:* each Class 1–3 teacher gets three short, lesson-specific observations with feedback.
>
> ## Steps
> 1. From the NIPUN guide, write a 5-item checklist for this week's lessons.
> 2. Each week, watch one lesson per Class 1–3 teacher, matched to that day's guide lesson. *Remember:* tell teachers in advance; this is support, not inspection.
> 3. Prepare using non-violent communication and active listening.
> 4. Note one strength and one specific next step about the lesson.
> 5. Talk with the teacher for 10 minutes the same day.
> 6. Repeat for 3 weeks; tick the checklist items you saw.
> 7. Write your reflections on DIKSHA.
>
> ## How you'll know it worked
> - Three observation-and-feedback cycles with every Class 1–3 teacher
> - More checklist items seen in week 3 than in week 1
> - Teachers rate the feedback as useful (anonymous 1–5)
>
> ## Before you start
> - Are the NIPUN (or state) lesson guides used daily in these classes? If not, this version doesn't apply.
>
> ## What changed from the original (and why)
> | Step | Change | Why |
> |---|---|---|
> | 1–2 | A short checklist from the day's guide lesson | Feedback on what the teacher is actually teaching |
> | 5–6 | A same-day talk, every week | Regular, specific feedback is what the coaching evidence is based on |
>
> Adapted from *School observation and feedback for teachers*, ShikshaLokam, via Shikshagraha Commons (#259). CC BY-SA.

## 8. What to watch for
Whether teachers experience it as support (the anonymous rating) · whether the HM can keep to the weekly rhythm · whether the checklist stays at 5 items.
