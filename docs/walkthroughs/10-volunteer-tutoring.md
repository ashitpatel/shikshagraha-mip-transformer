# Walkthrough 10 — "Community Chaupal" becomes in-school volunteer tutoring

> A **reference answer** written by hand, not tool output. Source: ShikshaLokam's *Domain-wise Micro Improvements Framework* (Commons #259), "Community Chaupal" (Head Teachers, 2 weeks), a real catalogue MIP. The context (many Class 3–5 non-readers; local youth and mothers willing to help) is assumed. Technical version: [evals/golden/catalogue-tutoring-chaupal.md](../../evals/golden/catalogue-tutoring-chaupal.md).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Runs it** | HM; teachers supervise; 3–6 community volunteers tutor |
| **Time** | 3 weeks (from 2) |
| **Cost** | Nil (letter and word cards, certificates) |
| **Methods applied** | **In-school tutoring** of the children furthest behind, with an **oral check before and after** |

## 1. The starting point
A one-off "chaupal": pick a date, choose activities, call community volunteers, assign them, inform parents on WhatsApp, run the session, collect feedback.

## 2. What it already does well
It already brings **community volunteers** into children's learning, which is the most promising starting point for tutoring.
**The gaps:** it's a **single event**, so there's no repeated practice. It isn't aimed at the children who need help most, has no simple structure for volunteers, no teacher supervision, and no way to see progress.

## 3. What was applied, and why

| Method | In plain words | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral check before and after** | Each Class 3–5 child reads letters, words and a sentence to the teacher; the teacher lists the 15–20 furthest behind; the same check at the end | So help goes to the children who need it, and you can see who moved up |
| **In-school volunteer tutoring** | 30 minutes every school day, in school hours; each volunteer takes a small group at the same level with a simple scripted activity; the teacher checks progress every week | In Indian municipal schools, local women tutoring the weakest Grade 3–4 children raised school scores by about 0.14 SD in year one and 0.28 SD in year two. Across 89 trials, tutoring averaged about 0.29 SD, and **in-school beat after-school**. *These results come from larger, longer trials; a 3-week school project should expect smaller gains, and this school's result is untested.* |

## 4. What was deliberately not done, and why
- **Not in the evening or at volunteers' homes.** In-school tutoring works better, and it keeps children safe. Volunteers work in supervised common areas, approved by the HM, with no one-to-one unsupervised time.
- **Volunteers don't teach new material.** They practise what teachers have introduced, using the scripted cards.
- **No "weak student" labels** in messages to parents or in front of other children.
- **Not just 2 weeks.** Repeated practice needs time; 3 weeks is the minimum, and the MIP suggests extending it if the endline shows movement.

## 5. Before → after

8 tasks · 2 added · 3 weeks.
**If no volunteers come forward:** trained Class 7–8 pupils run consolidation, under the same rules.

## 6. Safety and fairness rules
HM approves every volunteer · tutoring in school hours, in supervised common areas · no one-to-one unsupervised time · volunteers consolidate, never teach new content · the list of children stays with the teacher · no labels.

## 7. Teacher card (for the HM)

> # Community Chaupal: volunteers help children catch up in reading
> **Who runs it:** HM; teachers supervise · **Time:** 3 weeks · **Cost:** nil
> **Goal:** Bring community members and children together to encourage learning. *Added:* the children furthest behind in reading get 30 minutes of volunteer help every school day.
>
> ## Steps
> 1. Fix a daily 30-minute slot in school hours for 3 weeks.
> 2. Teachers listen to each Class 3–5 child read and list the 15–20 furthest behind. *Remember:* keep the list private.
> 3. Prepare simple activity cards for each level (letters, words, sentences).
> 4. Invite 3–6 local youth or mothers; approve each one.
> 5. Give each volunteer a small group at one level and a 30-minute briefing. *Remember:* volunteers practise, they don't teach new material.
> 6. Tell parents in person or by note that their child is getting extra reading help.
> 7. Each week, a teacher checks the attendance register and each group's progress, and regroups.
> 8. In week 3, repeat the reading check; thank the volunteers at assembly with certificates.
>
> ## How you'll know it worked
> - Every listed child has a "before" and "after" level
> - Tutoring happened on at least 80% of school days
> - At least 70% of tutored children move up a level
>
> ## Before you start
> - Are there at least 3 volunteers the HM can approve? If not, train Class 7–8 pupils instead.
>
> ## What changed from the original (and why)
> | Step | Change | Why |
> |---|---|---|
> | 1 | A daily slot instead of a one-off event | Children need repeated practice |
> | 2, 8 | A reading check before and after | Help goes to those who need it, and progress shows |
> | 4–5, 7 | Approved volunteers, small level groups, weekly teacher checks | This is the version with evidence behind it, and it keeps children safe |
>

## 8. What to watch for
Volunteer attendance (the register shows it) · whether the same children are always in the lowest group (they may need longer, or a different approach) · gains fading after the tutoring stops (seen in the Balsakhi trial), so plan whether to continue.
