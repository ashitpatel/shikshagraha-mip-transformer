# Walkthrough 4 — Learning Centre "Neighbourhood month": interest circles and everyday mentors

> A **reference answer** written by hand, not tool output. Source: Shikshagraha Commons resource **#753**, *Learning Centre Play Book* (Vidhya Vidhai Foundation; Katral Koodam Learning Centres, Coimbatore). Technical version: [evals/golden/learning-centre-playbook.md](../../evals/golden/learning-centre-playbook.md).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Runs it** | Learning Centre teacher-in-charge, with HT approval for visitors |

## 1. The starting point
The Play Book is a **year-long activity calendar** for Learning Centres set up because students were *gradually disengaging* before dropping out. This walkthrough takes its **August block** (Neighbourhood Explorer + Dream Job Map) as one MIP:
1. Prepare materials and the observation sheet
2. Run Dream Job Map; display the Dream Job Gallery
3. Run Neighbourhood Explorer
4. Build the Neighbourhood Learning Wall
5. Invite a local expert (scientist, artist, doctor, entrepreneur)
6. Record engagement in the observation framework

## 2. What it already does well
Hands-on making (storybooks, models, wall, exhibition) · peer teaching (Students as Teachers) · links to the neighbourhood and to careers · local experts.
**Correction:** an earlier proposal described the Play Book as "rigid, teacher-facilitated" layout guidance. It isn't.

**The gaps:** activities follow the calendar, so students never pursue *their own* interest over time; "experts" are distinguished professionals, not the everyday knowledge around students; families aren't an audience.

## 3. What was applied, and why

| Method | In plain words | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| **Interest over time** | Students name something they like or want to learn; they form circles of 3–5; every other week the circle chooses its own activity | The Play Book's own problem is disengagement; choice is the lever it lacks |
| **Everyday mentors** (relationships, connections across settings) | Each circle interviews one local knowledge holder: tailor, mechanic, farmer, shopkeeper, health worker, a parent with a craft | Students' own surroundings count as knowledge, and an adult takes their interest seriously |
| **Make something linking interest, mentor and subject** | E.g. a cost chart from the shopkeeper's prices | Connects the interest back to school learning |
| **Family showcase** | Circles present at a PTM or open day; mentors invited | A real audience, and families see the link |

## 4. What was deliberately not done, and why
- **The teacher stays accountable.** An earlier proposal moved tracking to a "student assembly". Students co-maintain a progress board, but the teacher owns the MIP.
- **No makerspace build-out.** It would add cost and scope; existing materials are used.
- **No platform or app tracking of mentors**, and no automated parent alerts from unverified tools.
- **No steering by caste or gender.** "Traditional trades" mustn't be matched to students' family backgrounds; each circle gets a mix of mentors and chooses.

## 5. Before → after

| # | Before | After | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prepare materials | Same, **plus privately flag low-engagement students** using the observation framework | changed |
| 2 | Dream Job Map | Same, **plus "one thing I like doing or want to learn"; form interest circles** | changed |
| 3 | — | **Circle choice periods every other week** | added |
| 4 | Neighbourhood Explorer | **Each circle interviews one everyday knowledge holder** (supervised) | changed |
| 5 | Learning Wall | **Each circle adds an artefact linking interest, mentor and a school subject** | changed |
| 6 | Invite an expert | **Family showcase at a PTM, mentors invited** | added (replaces the single expert visit) |
| 7 | Record engagement | Same, **plus two rows: "student chose the activity" and "community connection made"** | changed |

## 6. Safety and fairness rules
HT approves every visitor · visits supervised, in common areas · no one-to-one adult–child time · walks teacher-accompanied · flagged students not grouped together, and flags kept private · no photos of children online without written parental consent.

## 7. Teacher card

> # Neighbourhood Month: interest circles and everyday mentors
> **Who runs it:** Learning Centre teacher-in-charge · **Time:** 6 weeks · **Cost:** nil
> **Goal:** Students connect learning to their surroundings and careers. *Added:* every student pursues one interest they chose, and meets one everyday community person linked to it.
>
> ## Steps
> 1. Prepare materials; privately note students who seem disengaged.
> 2. In Dream Job Map, each student adds "one thing I like doing"; form circles of 3–5.
> 3. Every other week, each circle chooses its own Play Book activity.
> 4. Each circle interviews one local knowledge holder. *Remember:* HT approval, supervised, common areas.
> 5. Each circle adds an artefact to the Learning Wall linking interest, mentor and subject.
> 6. Family showcase at a PTM; invite the mentors.
> 7. Record engagement, including "chose own activity" and "community connection made".
>
> ## How you'll know it worked
> - At least 80% of flagged students attend at least 70% of circle periods
> - Every circle completes an interview and an artefact
> - Engagement ratings for flagged students improve from week 1 to week 6
>
> ## What changed from the original (and why)
> | Step | Change | Why |
> |---|---|---|
> | 2–3 | Interest circles and choice periods | Choice is the lever for students drifting away |
> | 4 | Everyday mentors | Students' own surroundings count as knowledge |
> | 6 | Family showcase | Families see what their children learn |
>

## 8. What to watch for
Whether flagged students actually join and stay · whether mentors from different backgrounds are offered · the link to dropout is a **hypothesis**, so don't report it as a result.
