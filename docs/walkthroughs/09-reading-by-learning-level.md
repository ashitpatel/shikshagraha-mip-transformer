# Walkthrough 9 — "Reading English", taught by learning level

> A **reference answer** written by hand, not tool output. Source: ShikshaLokam's *Domain-wise Micro Improvements Framework* (Commons #259), sheet *Cur & Ped & Ass*, "Reading English", a real catalogue MIP. The school context (Class 4–5, many non-readers, an HM willing to protect a period) is assumed for this example. Technical version: [evals/golden/catalogue-reading-by-level.md](../../evals/golden/catalogue-reading-by-level.md).

## 1. The starting point (catalogue MIP, 1 month)
1. Identify the reading levels of the class through reading activities
2. Word-recognition activities based on students' levels
3. Reading and understanding sentences
4. A creative activity for students to show their reading

## 2. What it already does well
It already **starts from students' levels** and **ends with children showing their reading**. That's the right instinct.
**The gaps:** levels are found informally and never re-checked; there's no **protected time**, so level work happens "when there's time"; nothing says children **move up a group** as they improve; nobody **reviews** progress during the month.

## 3. What was applied, and why

| Method | In plain words | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral check before and after** | Each child reads to the teacher for about 2 minutes: letters → simple words → a sentence → a short paragraph. Same check at the end. | Without a before-and-after check, you can't tell who moved. A teacher-made written test can roughly double the apparent gain, so a simple oral check is used. |
| **Teaching by learning level** | For a fixed 45 minutes every day, children work in groups by reading level, not by class, with activities for that level. They move up as soon as they're ready. | This is the practice with the strongest evidence in schools like these. In Indian government-school trials, a dedicated daily hour raised language scores by about 0.15 SD, and 40-day camps by about 0.70 SD. **Training teachers without protected time had no effect.** *These results come from larger trials; a one-month school project should expect smaller gains, and this school's result is untested.* |
| **Weekly review** | Every Friday, 10 minutes with the HM looking at *how many* children are at each level; regroup | Regular review is one of the parts that made the trials work |

## 4. What was deliberately not done, and why
- **No fixed "bright" and "weak" sections.** Groups are temporary and children move up. Permanent ability streaming shows no benefit and harms lower attainers.
- **No names on wall charts.** The chart shows counts per level; each child's level stays on the teacher's sheet.
- **Regular English lessons continue.** The level period is extra focused practice, not a replacement for the syllabus.
- **No "orient teachers on TaRL" step on its own.** A workshop without protected time and regrouping is exactly the version that showed no effect.
- **Connected Learning ideas were deferred, not dropped.** Interest-themed texts would also fit, but the essentials of level grouping used the room for changes. When the budget is tight, the practice with the strongest evidence goes first.

## 5. Before → after

| # | Before | After | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify reading levels | **One-to-one oral check; record levels; form level groups** | changed |
| 2 | — | **HM protects a daily 45-minute level period for the month, written on the timetable** | added |
| 3 | Word recognition by level | Same, **in level groups every day** (letter-sound games, word cards) | changed |
| 4 | Sentence reading | Same, for children at sentence level; **move children up as soon as they're ready** | changed |
| 5 | — | **Friday 10-minute review with the HM: counts per level, then regroup** | added |
| 6 | Creative reading activity | **Endline oral check**, then each child reads something at their level to a younger class or at a PTM | changed |

6 tasks · 2 added · 1 month · every essential element present.

## 6. Safety and fairness rules
Level records stay with the teacher · counts, not names, on any chart · groups mixed by gender, never called "weak" · regular lessons continue · progress judged by the oral check, not a written test.

## 7. Teacher card

> # Reading English, taught by learning level
> **Who runs it:** English teacher, with the HM · **Time:** 1 month · **Cost:** nil
> **Goal:** Build reading skills among students. *Added:* every child moves up at least one reading level (letter → word → sentence → paragraph).
>
> ## Steps
> 1. Listen to each child read for about 2 minutes (letters, words, a sentence, a short paragraph). Note their level on your sheet. Form level groups. *Remember:* keep the sheet private.
> 2. Agree with the HM a fixed 45-minute level period every day this month, and write it on the timetable. *Remember:* regular English lessons continue.
> 3. Every day, each group does activities at its level: letter-sound games, word cards, word chains.
> 4. Children at sentence level read and talk about sentences. Move any child up as soon as they're ready.
> 5. Every Friday, spend 10 minutes with the HM on a chart showing how many children are at each level. Regroup. *Remember:* numbers only, no names.
> 6. In the last week, repeat the oral check. Then each child reads something at their level to a younger class or at a PTM.
>
> ## How you'll know it worked
> - Every child has a "before" and "after" level
> - The level period happened on at least 80% of school days
> - At least 70% of children move up one or more levels
>
> ## Before you start
> - Can the HM protect the same 45 minutes every day this month? If not, plan a 10–20 day "reading camp" block instead.
>
> ## What changed from the original (and why)
> | Step | Change | Why |
> |---|---|---|
> | 1, 6 | A simple oral check before and after | So you can see who moved up |
> | 2 | A protected daily period | Without fixed time, this approach showed no effect in trials |
> | 4–5 | Move children up; review weekly | Flexible groups and regular review are what made it work |
>

## 8. What to watch for
Whether the period is really protected (the main way this fails) · whether some children stay in the lowest group all month, in which case add tutoring (see the in-school tutoring card) · whether the oral check is done the same way at the start and the end.
