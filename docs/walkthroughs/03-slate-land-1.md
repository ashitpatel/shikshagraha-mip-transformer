## 1. The starting point
SLATE is a **24-week programme** in which student leaders of house teams run English learning for their peers through four "lands": sounds → sight words → sentences → stories. This walkthrough takes **Land 1, Sound Island (6 weeks)** as one MIP:
1. Orient house leaders on SLATE and Land 1
2. Leaders run two 30-minute peer learning circles a week
3. House members run class-wise sound sessions
4. Keep the house points board
5. At week 6, check that at least 90% of each house know all the sounds

## 2. What it already does well
Strongly **peer-led** (student leaders run the circles), with **clear mastery checks** and later **student-made products** (sight-word charts, a reading corner, a final Learner's Mela of student stories).
**Correction:** an earlier proposal described SLATE as worksheets with tick-boxes. The source has neither.

**The gaps:** everyone works on the same content with no choice; Land 1 is drill with nothing made; the audience is the school only; houses compete, and nothing rewards helping the whole grade.

## 3. What was applied, and why

| Method | In plain words | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| **Interest** | Each house votes on a theme (cricket, farming, festivals, the market); the teacher adds 8–10 theme words covering the target sounds | Practice connects to what students care about, without changing *what* they practise |
| **Production** | Each week, the house makes a sound-hunt poster: theme pictures labelled by starting sound | Land 1 now produces something, not only drill |
| **Shared purpose** | Every house gets a bonus when the **whole grade** passes Land 1 | Houses have a reason to help each other, not only compete |
| **Connections across settings / audience** | Houses explain their posters to a younger class or to parents at the next PTM | A real audience beyond the classroom |

## 4. What was deliberately not done, and why
- **The phonics sequence and the 90% check are untouched.** This is foundational literacy, where structured, sequenced practice is the objective. Themes *add* words; they never replace the sounds.
- **No digital storytelling hub, podcasts or online gallery.** That would need devices, and children's voices or images online need written parental consent under India's DPDP Act 2023. Products are paper and spoken.
- **No invented evidence.** An earlier proposal said "Ito's research shows retention triples". No such finding exists, and it was removed.
- **Not the whole 24 weeks.** Lands 2–4 follow as separate MIPs with the same pattern (a theme-word strip; a house mini-book; a family Story Mela).

## 6. Safety and fairness rules
The theme vote is open to all house members, not just leaders · students not yet reading contribute drawings and speaking · no photos of children shared outside school without written parental consent.

## 7. Teacher card

> # SLATE Land 1: Sound Island, with house interest themes
> **Who runs it:** English teacher · **Time:** 6 weeks · **Cost:** nil
> **Goal:** At least 90% of each house know all English sounds by week 6. *Added:* each house makes 6 weekly posters on a theme it chose.
>
> ## Steps
> 1. Orient house leaders on SLATE and Land 1.
> 2. Each house votes on a theme; list 8–10 theme words covering the sounds. *Remember:* themes add words, never replace the sounds.
> 3. Leaders run two 30-minute circles a week.
> 4. Class sessions end each week with a house sound-hunt poster. *Make:* a poster.
> 5. House points as usual, plus a bonus for every house when the whole grade passes.
> 6. Week-6 check: at least 90% per house.
> 7. Poster walk for a younger class or parents. *Remember:* no photos online without written consent.
>
> ## How you'll know it worked
> - At least 90% per house know all sounds (no lower than before)
> - 6 posters per house; at least one poster walk
>
> ## What changed from the original (and why)
> | Step | Change | Why |
> |---|---|---|
> | 2 | Houses choose a theme | Practice links to students' interests |
> | 4 | A weekly poster | Students make something every week |
> | 5 | A whole-grade bonus | Houses help each other, not only compete |
> | 7 | A poster walk | A real audience beyond the class |
>

## 8. What to watch for
**Mastery rates must not drop.** If they do, the additions are taking time from practice. Also watch that themes don't become the point, and that leaders don't pick the theme for everyone.
