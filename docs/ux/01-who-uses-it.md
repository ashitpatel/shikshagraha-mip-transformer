# Who uses the MIP Transformer

> **Status:** design intent. No user interface exists yet. The pipeline (prompts, schema, cards) has been written but not run live. This describes how the tool is meant to be used, so it can be reviewed before anything is built.

The tool takes a **Micro-Improvement Project (MIP)** from Shikshagraha Commons and, only where it fits, offers a revised version that applies **evidence-based practices** (teaching by learning level, in-school tutoring, a daily sound-symbol reading block, head-teacher feedback on state lesson guides: the methods with the strongest trial evidence in schools like these), **Connected Learning** (learning that connects students' interests, relationships and opportunities) or **participatory budgeting** (a group deciding together how to use a shared resource). It always keeps the original available.

## The four kinds of user

### 1. MIP author: NGO programme staff, DIET faculty, a district resource person
- **What they do today:** write or adapt MIPs for schools in their programme, often from the Commons catalogue.
- **What they want:** better versions of the MIPs they hand out, without making them longer or harder to run.
- **How they use the tool:** pick a catalogue MIP or a Commons resource → get a revised version with an explanation → edit it if needed → publish it to their schools alongside the original.
- **What they need to trust it:** to see *why* each change was made, what was deliberately **not** changed, and that nothing unsafe or unsupported was added.

### 2. MIP owner: head teacher (HM/HT)
- **Owns about 60% of catalogue MIPs** (management, infrastructure, community).
- **What they want:** something they can finish in 2–4 weeks with what they have.
- **How they use the tool:** see two versions of a MIP (original and revised), read a one-page teacher card for each, answer any open question (e.g. "Is a discretionary amount confirmed?"), choose one, run it, report back.
- **What matters:** the choice stays theirs. Plain language, no framework jargon, nothing that needs new devices or money.

### 3. MIP owner: teacher
- **Owns about 27% of catalogue MIPs** (mostly pedagogy: reading, maths, projects).
- **What they want:** classroom activities that keep the syllabus on track and engage students who are drifting.
- **How they use the tool:** same as the HM, usually for reading, maths and pedagogy MIPs, where evidence-based practices and Connected Learning changes are most likely.
- **What matters:** the core teaching (e.g. the phonics sequence) isn't replaced; added tasks fit in existing periods.

### 4. School Management Committee (SMC)
- **Not a direct user of the tool**, but the decision-making body when a revised MIP adds a prioritisation vote or a knapsack ballot.
- **What matters:** ballots *inform* the committee's agreement and don't override it; women members are heard; results are posted publicly.

## Who the tool is **not** for
- **Students** never own a MIP and never see the tool. They get roles *inside* tasks (choosing a theme, being a buddy, voting on a school space).
- **Central allocation:** the tool doesn't decide which school or classroom gets which intervention (see EXPLORATION §11).
